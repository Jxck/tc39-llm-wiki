## Vision for numeric types in ECMAScript

- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1Uzrf-IwPrljF2BhCbCWuwQxlgGSm_bcd3FRbPO3Yrio/edit#slide=id.p)

[SFC](../../people/SFC.md) gave a holistic framing of the number space, synthesizing five problem spaces from conversations with delegates: [decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md) arithmetic (0.1 + 0.2 = 0.3), representing precision (1 star vs 1.0 stars pluralizes differently; round-tripping with precision-retaining systems like decimal128), more significant digits (beyond ~15.95: large financial values, ML weight differences), unergonomic footguns, and associating a dimension/unit with a number. He stressed a non-issue: representing [decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md) _values_ is already possible in `Number` — the problems arrive with [decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md) _arithmetic_ and normalization. Six solution shapes were surveyed: the Measure proposal, IEEE decimal128 with precision (surprising precision propagation; the 2.5m vs 2.50m equality question), normalized decimal128 without precision (wasteful bits, not interoperable), a new DecimalMeasure composition, `Number.prototype` [decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md) methods with a TypeScript/JSSugar operator layer ([RBN](../../people/RBN.md): TypeScript will not type-directed-emit `+` — only a dedicated operator like `~+` is statically transpilable), and a scaled BigInt ("[decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md) BigInt", feasibility unconfirmed; [WH](../../people/WH.md) recalled the old BigDecimal runaway-precision concerns). Opinions diverged: [MM](../../people/MM.md) argued IEEE's trailing-zeros notion of precision matches no coherent use case ("how many sixes would you write down before you wrote down the seven?" — display precision is total digits, usually static, not data-carried trailing zeros, with [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) concurring that Intl needs the number _plus_ trailing zeros but not their propagation across operations); [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) hoped for [Temporal](../../proposals/temporal.md)-style distinct types you can't misuse; [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) held a primitive [decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md) is the right long-term solution and a partial solution without a path there makes things worse; [JMN](../../people/JMN.md) ranked solutions 3, 2, 6 (keeping Measure and [Decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md) separable); [WH](../../people/WH.md) called the bit-pattern-efficiency argument a distraction (base-1000 waste is larger).

The conclusion was discussion only: number-related champions to keep iterating toward a clean, future-proof architecture across the [decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md)/measure/precision proposals.

## Measure Stage 1 update

- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/tc39-transfer/proposal-measure)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/17ypyikW1q8RFf5AnnYKpe5dsdrHTb0BnSzZGaq0mm-I/edit?usp=sharing)

[EAO](../../people/EAO.md) (presenting in place of [BAN](../../people/BAN.md), on medical leave) presented the Measure strawman: separating the "what" (a number plus a unit/dimension) from the "how" (formatting options), supporting mixed-unit formatting ("3 ft, 6 in") and unit conversion, with the key spec-level move being a new primordial object that `Intl.NumberFormat` can format directly — motivated by `Intl.MessageFormat` needing a first-class currency/unit value a translator can't mangle, and by stable-formatting concerns (developers will otherwise parse Intl output to recover these values). Explicitly out of scope: unit formatting itself (exists), localized unit formatting (exists), arbitrary digit counts, currency conversion, and primitives/operator overloading. Questions posed to TG1: should extra unit data be added, should locale/usage-based conversions be in the initial proposal (e.g. person-height, ~2-4KB of CLDR data, value-dependent below 2.5 years), should Measure support arithmetic operators — hinging on the big question: should Measure care about anything beyond formatting and conversion? [WH](../../people/WH.md) pressed that precision handling is the long pole: a presentation of the precision considerations is needed before deciding whether conversion and arithmetic share one treatment or two that could drift. [MM](../../people/MM.md) established (with [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) agreeing) that Measure must carry its own precision theory regardless — `Number` and BigInt carry none — and that no coherent theory tracks only trailing zeros.

The conclusion was no clear committee opinions on the scope questions; further discussion on the representation and handling of precision was requested.

## Continuation: Error Stacks Structure for Stage 2

- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-error-stacks)

[JHD](../../people/JHD.md) broke the proposal into three separable pieces — the normative-optional `stack` accessor, a `getStackString` static (usable compatibly with hardened-JS censoring), and the structured-data `getStack` (the bulk of the value, but only useful tied to the string contents). [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) returned with the 2019 record: SpiderMonkey already tried aligning its stack space with V8 and broke internal code, extensions, and the web; standardizing the schema without the contents is not merely useless but **harmful** — it increases usage of stack inspection, exposing non-interoperable differences to a broader audience. [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) agreed: standardizing a new accessor alongside the existing non-interoperable machinery is a net increase in non-interop surface — though his concern is the structure part, not the first two pieces (which he just doesn't see the value of). [MM](../../people/MM.md) argued piece 1 alone is hugely valuable for censoring (JSC pre-endows an own `stack` data property with no hook to intervene; V8's recently-added own accessor with an identical getter/setter is an unpluggable capability leak since the setter honours non-strings), and offered compromises in both directions: "any frame may be omitted, but any frame that is present reflects reality" (which he doubts engines will accept) and splitting off just the accessor. [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) was frank that standardizing would not force V8 to unship or change its non-standard behavior ("We haven't done this investigation because we don't think it's high priority. And you don't get to force that high priority by making it a proposal") but did not object to the split; [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) said Mozilla would welcome specification of the accessor, which causes web-compat problems today.

The conclusion was a split: a new proposal for just the stack accessor (which became [Error Stack Accessor](../../proposals/error-stack-accessor.md), [MM](../../people/MM.md) co-championing) to be brought back for Stage 1, with the existing proposal renamed/refocused on the structure and its README documenting these concerns.

## Continuation: import defer updates

- wiki: [import defer](../../proposals/import-defer.md)
- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-defer-import-eval/)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1yFbqn6px5rIwAVjBbXgrYgql1L90tKPTWZq2A5D6f5Q/)

[NRO](../../people/NRO.md) ran the deferred slide-7 temperature check from Tuesday: should _any_ query about a module's exports (not just `Get`, but `Object.keys`-style key listing and `in` checks) trigger evaluation? For: it aligns the semantics with what transpilers/bundlers can actually implement, where most ESM runs. Against: it removes the user-facing ability to list exports without evaluating, and is driven entirely by tools' needs, not engines. The temperature check came back 3 strong positive / 9 positive / 3 following / 1 indifferent ([GB](../../people/GB.md) absent — [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) read his message on making such tradeoffs openly and proceeded to ask for consensus), and consensus was reached. Recapping the Tuesday session: consensus was also in hand for hiding `then` on deferred namespace objects and the `"deferred module"` `toStringTag`; the `Symbol.evaluated` evaluated-state probe got supportive feedback but no consensus was asked, given [Stabilize](../../proposals/stabilize.md) works in the same area.

The conclusion was consensus for the key-list evaluation trigger (completing the three changes); the `Symbol.evaluated` follow-up remains unlanded.
