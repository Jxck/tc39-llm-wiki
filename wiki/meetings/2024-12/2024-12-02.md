## More Currency Display Choices

- wiki: [More Currency Display Choices](../../proposals/more-currency-display-choices.md)
- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/eemeli/proposal-intl-currency-display-choices)

[EAO](../../people/EAO.md) presented a small `Intl.NumberFormat` extension adding two `currencyDisplay` option values: `'formalSymbol'` (always the longer form, e.g. "US$" for USD even in en-US, addressing the case where the short "symbol" form is unavailable) and `'never'` (format with currency rules, such as fractional-digit defaults, but display no currency indicator at all). ICU already supports both behaviors, which is where the "formal" name comes from, though a TG2 discussion left `'formalSymbol'` vs `'wideSymbol'` open. [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) confirmed his understanding that there was no further design space here, and supported Stage 2 on that basis; [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) and [DE](../../people/DE.md) also supported.

The conclusion was Stage 2, with [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) and [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) as committed Stage 2.7 reviewers.

## Upsert (formerly Map.emplace) Update and request for Stage 2 reviewers

- wiki: [Upsert](../../proposals/upsert.md)
- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-upsert)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/15sWTvdWIo9Jt12LFRNBPJo1N_8xsMSCB3jy73HBFX-M/)

[DLM](../../people/DLM.md) presented the proposal renamed back to "upsert" (MF's point: name problems, not solutions), with two methods — `Map.prototype.getOrInsert` and `getOrInsertComputed` (the latter taking a callback to defer computing the default value). The main design question was what to do when the callback mutates the map by inserting the key itself: throw, or accept it. [KG](../../people/KG.md) and [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) both leaned toward the non-throwing variant (re-check the key after the callback and set the callback's returned value), [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) on pay-as-you-go grounds — a throw would require a mutation check on every map operation, and the existence-check heuristic can't detect delete-and-re-add anyway. [RBN](../../people/RBN.md) agreed, noting iteration doesn't throw on mutation either. Bloomberg (via [MF](../../people/MF.md), relaying [ACE](../../people/ACE.md)) preferred non-throwing because throwing catches only one specific case of mutation.

The conclusion was: committee in favour of the non-throwing solution to issue #40 ([PR #71](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-upsert/pull/71)), naming to be resolved in issue #60, and [JMN](../../people/JMN.md) and [MF](../../people/MF.md) volunteered as Stage 2 reviewers.

## `Intl.DurationFormat` for Stage 4

- wiki: [Intl.DurationFormat](../../proposals/intl-durationformat.md)
- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/tc39/ecma402/pull/943)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1bAuZ0ZSSYUdJxiDYXz2tUWHZwaOmYkNoLpQBBy_qz1w/edit?usp=sharing)

[USA](../../people/USA.md) (presenting work championed largely by [BAN](../../people/BAN.md)) asked for Stage 4 for `Intl.DurationFormat`, a locale-aware formatter for composite durations with per-unit width control (long/narrow/digital — the Skyscanner use case mixes widths per unit). The proposal reached Stage 1 in 2020-02, Stage 2 in 2020-06, and Stage 3 in 2021-10 (before Stage 2.7 existed); the long Stage 3 reflected implementer feedback and building the API before validating it in tooling. Test262 tests shipped, two compatible implementations passed them (thanks to [YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md), [ABL](../../people/ABL.md), [FYT](../../people/FYT.md)), MDN documentation exists, and the ECMA-402 PR was approved by TG2. [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) supported with his Temporal hat on — DurationFormat will be the way to format `Temporal.Duration` once Temporal lands.

The conclusion was Stage 4, with supporting comments from [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) and [PFC](../../people/PFC.md).

## Iterator helpers close receiver on argument validation failure

- wiki: [Iterator helpers](../../proposals/iterator-helpers.md)
- proposal: [spec PR #3467](https://github.com/tc39/ecma262/pull/3467)

[KG](../../people/KG.md) presented a needs-consensus PR fixing an oversight in the just-landed iterator helpers: when an argument fails validation (`filter`/`map` given a non-callable, `take`/`drop` given a bad number), the helper does not close its receiver iterator, violating the general contract that whoever receives an iterator is responsible for closing it. [MF](../../people/MF.md) strongly supported ("we didn't think of the `this` value as a parameter here"), and [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) supported.

The conclusion was approval, with tests to be written before merging.

## The importance of supporting materials

- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1teo8pAE4lbFTIlPZxum2MBcNZfGdUM2Y8huEiVdvQiQ/)

[DLM](../../people/DLM.md) reminded the committee that supporting materials (slides, motivation, use cases) are already part of the process for advancement, and that implementers review every proposal and need lead time to consult the right experts — without materials they are "left guessing what is actually going to be presented," which has led to withheld consensus. He floated requiring at least initial materials before the agenda deadline and moving the deadline earlier, while explicitly not asking for a process change now. [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) pushed back that a hard requirement creates perverse incentives (late-inspired slides would be banned, proposals without materials would just wing it); [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) suggested marking late slide additions and noted Igalia's internal earlier deadline; [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) supported the spirit — more material earlier improves the chances of a good reception.

The conclusion was no process change requested — a reminder of the importance of supporting materials for implementers.

## re-using IteratorResult objects in iterator helpers

- wiki: [Iterator helpers](../../proposals/iterator-helpers.md)
- proposal: [PR #3489](https://github.com/tc39/ecma262/pull/3489)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1HQzC15dFnQClnUWYHSFx95aMuiJjHAjE186flPW7iZE)

[MF](../../people/MF.md) presented a needs-consensus PR to have the iterator helpers (`take`, `drop`, `filter`) reuse the underlying iterator's `IteratorResult` objects instead of copying `value` into fresh objects — matching what `yield*` does in the spec (inconsistently implemented; JSC and LibJS create new objects). The question needed settling before [iterator sequencing](../../proposals/iterator-sequencing.md) could go for Stage 3, since ABL's test262 PR for `Iterator.concat` asserts one behavior or the other. [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) opposed for `Iterator.prototype` helpers: even `take`/`drop` must read `done`, so getter-induced weirdness remains, generators can't shim it, and extra result properties would be inconsistently visible. A temperature check leaned negative for the prototype helpers.

The conclusion was to reject reuse for the existing `Iterator.prototype` helpers but reuse for `Iterator.concat` (positively received in a second check) — [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) noted the inconsistency is what makes a generator-based `Iterator.concat` polyfill possible.

## ShadowRealm for Stage 3

- wiki: [ShadowRealm](../../proposals/shadowrealm.md)
- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-shadowrealm)
- slide: [link](https://ptomato.name/talks/tc39-2024-12)

[PFC](../../people/PFC.md) recapped how the "which Web APIs exist in a ShadowRealm" question had been answered since the 2023 demotion: a new W3C TAG design principle ("expose everywhere" for purely computational features — no I/O, no user-agent state, nothing relying on an event loop, expose conservatively), a settled HTML integration PR awaiting two explicit positive implementation signals, and web-platform-tests running in ShadowRealms created from window/worker/shared worker/service worker/audio worklet/another ShadowRealm, with Mozilla's test-coverage concerns resolved ([DLM](../../people/DLM.md) confirmed on the record). [KG](../../people/KG.md) probed edge cases — `crypto.subtle` failing the "purely computational" test, WebGPU, audio worklet allocation rules — which [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) took as follow-ups rather than blockers; [KKL](../../people/KKL.md) reported Agoric unworried since realm globals remain deletable by first-running code. Then the discussion turned normative: [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) argued browser delegates should not grant Stage 3 without internal consensus from their DOM/HTML teams ("Stage 3 means it's coming for sure"), and both [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) and [KM](../../people/KM.md) reported their HTML-side teams had no objections but also no statement of interest in implementing.

The conclusion was that [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) withdrew the Stage 3 request — champions will get the WPTs merged, look at `crypto.subtle`, and obtain explicit commitments from the browser DOM teams before returning (targeting the next meeting).
