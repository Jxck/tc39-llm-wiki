## AsyncContext Stage 2 updates

- wiki: [AsyncContext](../../proposals/async-context.md)
- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-async-context/)
- slide: [slides](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1ok6fX9PN3XEv9ZwffrDzJX24uuiNrkGDZN-KgGwGkc0/edit?usp=sharing)

JRL gave the Stage 2 update on AsyncContext. Changes include bringing back the `AsyncContext.wrap` static, taking FinalizationRegistry callback snapshots at construction time, snapshotting the context at generator initialization (spec-internal generators exempt), and fixing the context reported to unhandledrejection. Implementation strategies (map vs linked list vs HAMT) were discussed and around 150 Test262 tests exist. HTML integration is the blocker for a Stage 2.7 ask.

The conclusion was no advancement — HTML integration must land first.

## Deferred import evaluation for Stage 2.7 (without "tree-shakeable" exports)

- wiki: [import defer](../../proposals/import-defer.md)
- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-defer-import-eval/)
- slide: [slides](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1oPEF8nA9Iq5cAqjN-FqMigNNfz6lWCUbNfIsEjRXf4Y/)

NRO presented the latest shape of `import defer`: module evaluation is deferred until any string property is accessed on the namespace, which also removes the Wasm top-level-await workaround, with case studies from BlueSky (~30% faster startup) and Element (~50%). KG noted the benefit only exists for modules with side effects, and SYG objected that a top-level await silently disabling deferral is "wild" — he would prefer an error; the value for browsers vs bundlers was debated.

The conclusion was that no stage advancement was asked for — the top-level-await interaction remains the open question.

JWK presented Iterator.range for Stage 2.7, but it was blocked: MM wants the range restricted to safe integers while KG and WH argued for fractional support, citing decades of fractional-range experience in other languages; the usefulness of BigInt ranges was debated and resolved in favor of keeping BigInt. ## `Math.sumExact` for stage 2.7

- wiki: [Math.sumPrecise](../../proposals/math-sum-precise.md)
- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-math-sum)
- slide: [slides](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1QallvKcuIL2UHALEYnP4AdT8nX_iZ6QHDgpVISUXHMg)

KG presented Math.sumExact for Stage 2.7, renamed to Math.sumPrecise at MM's suggestion. It takes an iterable of Numbers with no coercion, returns -0 for an empty iterable, and — after an 8-5 temperature check — the champion decided a NaN input drains the rest of the iterator before returning NaN (no "from"-suffixed variant). WH's algorithms confirmed full precision is practical to specify.

The conclusion was Stage 2.7 as Math.sumPrecise.

## Discussing new directions for R&T

- wiki: [Records & Tuples](../../proposals/records-and-tuples.md)
- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-record-tuple)
- slide: [slides](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1JfChmW8tQ2_mrFDynosNqa1tjJ2j-qX6WoKm8vc_tkY/)
- proposal: [related repo](https://github.com/acutmore/proposal-keyby)

ACE presented new directions for Records & Tuples: rethink the feature as object/array-like containers without `===` overloading or new primitive types, motivated by composite keys for Maps/Sets. Discussion covered `uniqueBy`-style hooks versus a hash+equals model (RBN floated an `Object.hash` idea), SYG asked about the GC implications of Records as WeakMap keys, and MAH objected to changing the behavior of existing collections without opt-in. EAO reported Mozilla saw it as a good Stage 1-level conversation.

The conclusion was no conclusions — exploration continues.
