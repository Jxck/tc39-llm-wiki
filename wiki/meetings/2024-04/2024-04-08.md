## update on automatically producing print-quality PDFs

- slide: [slides](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1kCDZewoWZtL26FnFSiK4tr6ZotGo7TR0_AZGlCwHiEQ/edit)

MF reported on the Paged.js experiment to replace AWB's yearly manual print layout of the spec. Headers, footers, page numbering, and the table of contents work, but Paged.js does not support the page-breaking rules Ecma's TOOLS-011 standard requires, and its layout bugs can silently drop table rows or algorithm steps — so a Paged.js PDF cannot be treated as canonical. Fixing everything by hand was estimated at 50-100 hours per year, work the editor group is unwilling to repeat annually; a professional layout service ($1,000-5,000 per year) was MF's preferred option, with sponsoring Paged.js improvements (KG's suggestion) as an alternative. The conclusion was that AWB will do the 2024 PDFs one more time with significantly reduced manual work thanks to MF's groundwork, and TC39 will ask Ecma to budget a long-term solution for future years.

## Joint-iteration: confirm our stance on issue 1

- wiki: [Joint Iteration](../../proposals/joint-iteration.md)
- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-joint-iteration)
- proposal: [issue #1](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-joint-iteration/issues/1#issuecomment-1981587641)

MF reopened the question of whether arrays should be part of joint iteration (an array-side zip) or whether `Iterator.zip(...).toArray()` covers the need. JHD argued for arrays — less boilerplate and easy refactoring between arrays and iterators — while KG was not convinced the mental-overhead argument suffices, MM weighed the cognitive burden, and SYG cautioned against assuming array methods are faster and against a blanket policy of mirroring iterator methods on arrays.

The conclusion was no resolution: discussion continues on issue #1.
