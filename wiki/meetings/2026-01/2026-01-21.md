## Composable value-backed accessors for Stage 1

- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/LeaVerou/proposal-composable-accessors)
- slide: [link](https://projects.verou.me/proposal-composable-value-accessors/slides/)

[LVU](../../people/LVU.md) (OpenJS) presented a follow-up to the class fields introspection proposal that failed to get Stage 1 in November 2025: fields were held to be internal implementation details, but there was general agreement that classes need a way to explicitly declare the data properties that are part of their public API. With first-class protocols excluding fields (they can neither satisfy nor provide them), protocols can currently be satisfied only through accessors and methods, so she framed two problem statements: (1) a high signal-to-noise way to declare public data properties that become part of the class shape, and (2) better DX for the very common "additive" accessor patterns — validation, normalization, side effects — where today the accessor plumbing must be rebuilt from scratch. She sketched a base-plus-layers model (the base sugars to a plain accessor; the layers could be built-in [decorators](../../proposals/decorators.md)), questioned whether "accessor" is even the right mental frame (citing an informal poll where 7 of 10 JavaScript authors could not explain the term), and showed Swift's `willSet`/`didSet` as prior art. The discussion pushed back hard: [RBN](../../people/RBN.md) argued the [decorators](../../proposals/decorators.md) and grouped/auto accessors proposals already cover about 90% of the use cases (the `accessor` keyword exists precisely to mark the data-property-to-accessor transformation, per implementer requirements) and defended the keyword choice against `property`/`data`; [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) opposed the syntax path as "too much syntax for a much narrower use" and called obscuring that user code runs on get/set an anti-goal; [KM](../../people/KM.md) detailed the performance reality (accessors are 2-3x slower than data properties outside the optimizing JIT tiers, which most code never reaches) and warned against syntax that hides the cost; [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) and [RBR](../../people/RBR.md) saw no problem worth the complexity; [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) supported problem statement 1 enthusiastically but not 2; [SHS](../../people/SHS.md) supported problem statement 2 for the sake of well-known tooling-visible solutions; [CM](../../people/CM.md) remained unconvinced ("it has a flavor of the solution looking for a problem") while allowing that a standard set of built-in [decorators](../../proposals/decorators.md) divorced from the accessor question is an interesting idea; [WH](../../people/WH.md) was unclear what new problem area this explores. [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) summarized the precedent: Stage 1 can be granted with the feedback recorded — multiple delegates do not want syntax and are unconvinced of the problem statements, all of which must be addressed before Stage 2.

The conclusion was to continue the conversation after the lunch break (see the continuation below); no consensus was called in the morning session.

## Continuation: Composable value-backed accessors for Stage 1

- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/LeaVerou/proposal-composable-accessors)
- slide: [link](https://projects.verou.me/proposal-composable-value-accessors/slides/)

Returning after lunch, [LVU](../../people/LVU.md) recapped the morning's feedback — consensus against solving this via new syntax, agreement that auto accessors cover value-backed public data properties, strong interest in built-in [decorators](../../proposals/decorators.md), and mild interest in alias accessors for forwarding — and revised her position to favor built-in [decorators](../../proposals/decorators.md) as the first step: cheaper to implement, lower risk, a low-fi way to test the waters, and preserving the option of syntax later. She cited [NRO](../../people/NRO.md)'s accessor usage analysis shared during the break (75% property forwarding, 15% lazy initial computation, 10% validation) as evidence forwarding is the dominant pattern. The work was then split: composable accessors via built-in [decorators](../../proposals/decorators.md) reached Stage 1 with support from [SHS](../../people/SHS.md), [RBN](../../people/RBN.md), [PFC](../../people/PFC.md), and [MF](../../people/MF.md). For alias accessors as a separate exploratory proposal, [RBR](../../people/RBR.md) objected that the benefit is too small and getters/setters are best kept explicit, but withdrew the objection after [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) (no committee consensus that using accessors is a mistake) and [CM](../../people/CM.md) (the narrowing satisfies his earlier concerns) pushed back; [JHX](../../people/JHX.md), [CDA](../../people/CDA.md), and [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) supported it.

The conclusion was Stage 1 for composable accessors via built-in [decorators](../../proposals/decorators.md) and Stage 1 for alias accessors, with the alias-accessor track proceeding as exploration to validate motivation and design space before any further advancement.
