## Composable value-backed accessors for Stage 1

- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/LeaVerou/proposal-composable-accessors)
- slide: [link](https://projects.verou.me/proposal-composable-value-accessors/slides/)

LVU (OpenJS) presented a follow-up to the class fields introspection proposal that failed to get Stage 1 in November 2025: fields were held to be internal implementation details, but there was general agreement that classes need a way to explicitly declare the data properties that are part of their public API. With first-class protocols excluding fields (they can neither satisfy nor provide them), protocols can currently be satisfied only through accessors and methods, so she framed two problem statements: (1) a high signal-to-noise way to declare public data properties that become part of the class shape, and (2) better DX for the very common "additive" accessor patterns — validation, normalization, side effects — where today the accessor plumbing must be rebuilt from scratch. She sketched a base-plus-layers model (the base sugars to a plain accessor; the layers could be built-in decorators), questioned whether "accessor" is even the right mental frame (citing an informal poll where 7 of 10 JavaScript authors could not explain the term), and showed Swift's `willSet`/`didSet` as prior art. The discussion pushed back hard: RBN argued the decorators and grouped/auto accessors proposals already cover about 90% of the use cases (the `accessor` keyword exists precisely to mark the data-property-to-accessor transformation, per implementer requirements) and defended the keyword choice against `property`/`data`; RGN opposed the syntax path as "too much syntax for a much narrower use" and called obscuring that user code runs on get/set an anti-goal; KM detailed the performance reality (accessors are 2-3x slower than data properties outside the optimizing JIT tiers, which most code never reaches) and warned against syntax that hides the cost; DLM and RBR saw no problem worth the complexity; PFC supported problem statement 1 enthusiastically but not 2; SHS supported problem statement 2 for the sake of well-known tooling-visible solutions; CM remained unconvinced ("it has a flavor of the solution looking for a problem") while allowing that a standard set of built-in decorators divorced from the accessor question is an interesting idea; WH was unclear what new problem area this explores. JHD summarized the precedent: Stage 1 can be granted with the feedback recorded — multiple delegates do not want syntax and are unconvinced of the problem statements, all of which must be addressed before Stage 2.

The conclusion was to continue the conversation after the lunch break (see the continuation below); no consensus was called in the morning session.

## Continuation: Composable value-backed accessors for Stage 1

- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/LeaVerou/proposal-composable-accessors)
- slide: [link](https://projects.verou.me/proposal-composable-value-accessors/slides/)

Returning after lunch, LVU recapped the morning's feedback — consensus against solving this via new syntax, agreement that auto accessors cover value-backed public data properties, strong interest in built-in decorators, and mild interest in alias accessors for forwarding — and revised her position to favor built-in decorators as the first step: cheaper to implement, lower risk, a low-fi way to test the waters, and preserving the option of syntax later. She cited NRO's accessor usage analysis shared during the break (75% property forwarding, 15% lazy initial computation, 10% validation) as evidence forwarding is the dominant pattern. The work was then split: composable accessors via built-in decorators reached Stage 1 with support from SHS, RBN, PFC, and MF. For alias accessors as a separate exploratory proposal, RBR objected that the benefit is too small and getters/setters are best kept explicit, but withdrew the objection after PFC (no committee consensus that using accessors is a mistake) and CM (the narrowing satisfies his earlier concerns) pushed back; JHX, CDA, and PFC supported it.

The conclusion was Stage 1 for composable accessors via built-in decorators and Stage 1 for alias accessors, with the alias-accessor track proceeding as exploration to validate motivation and design space before any further advancement.
