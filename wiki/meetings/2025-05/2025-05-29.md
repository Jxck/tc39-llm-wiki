## IDL for ECMAScript with WebIDL

- proposal: [proposal-idl](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-idl)

TFA (Tooru Fujisawa, Mozilla) presented proposal-idl: describing ECMAScript built-ins in WebIDL so bindings, types, and tooling can be generated from the spec (a prototype exists). It is purely editorial, but needs notation extensions (data `property` syntax, non-enumerable and `length` attributes, `@@symbol` syntax, `NoBrandCheck`, etc.), with three integration plans: an appendix, a dual view, or fully integrated text. [TAB](../../people/TAB.md) warned that APIs needing heavy extended-attribute annotation would mean failure; [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) and [MF](../../people/MF.md) cautioned against changing language design for IDL's sake; [WH](../../people/WH.md) preferred one source generating both formats; [LCA](../../people/LCA.md) raised the legal question of normatively referencing a WHATWG spec from an Ecma standard; [CZW](../../people/CZW.md) said Node prefers explicit steps.

The conclusion was that none was sought — the speaker's summary records the next steps, with a preference for integration path 2 (the dual view).

## ECMAScript Framework

- slide: [link](https://github.com/tc39/agendas/blob/main/2025/Framework_Ecma_TC39_2025.pdf)

SHN (Samina Husain, Ecma) explained the Ecma framework TC39 operates in — membership and fees, governance, and voting — responding to questions raised during the previous meeting's consensus discussion. Only Ecma ordinary members hold formal voting rights; invited experts participate fully in discussion and are highly valued but do not vote. Ecma recommends three weeks of lead time for official documents, but TC39's 10-day agenda rule is acceptable if all stakeholders are treated fairly.

The conclusion, per the speaker's summary: general support for keeping TC39's current efficient processes, with openness to longer review periods; SHN will bring material on consensus best practices and possibly IPR/royalty-free policy to a future plenary.

## Moving TCQ forward

- proposal: [tcq (reloaded)](https://github.com/zalari/tcq/tree/reloaded)
- slide: [link](https://cloud.zalari.de/s/g4HNCjSWJHcKz4Q)

CHU (Christian Ulbrich) presented "TCQ reloaded": the old TCQ code made provider-agnostic, auto-deployed via GitHub Actions to tcq-reloaded.com and restartable by anyone, as a stopgap while a modern rewrite with the same concepts is built. [MF](../../people/MF.md) strongly supported the rewrite with an eye to maintainability; [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) warned against hosting that depends on one delegate's account, and [RPR](../../people/RPR.md) suggested requesting Ecma funding (under $100 a month) via ExeCom.

The conclusion was general agreement (not consensus-seeking) to try TCQ reloaded at the next plenary, falling back to the old TCQ if needed, with CHU coordinating development and the rewrite.

## Continuation: `Decimal` Stage 1 Update

- wiki: [Decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md)
- proposal: [proposal-decimal](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-decimal/)

[JMN](../../people/JMN.md) resumed the [Decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md) `Decimal.Amount` discussion. [WH](../../people/WH.md)'s questions exposed that rounding guarantees only hold for Decimal128-representable values and that ties-to-even gives odd results (0.95 at one significant digit). On `Decimal.Amount.prototype.equals`, [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) wanted it for hash collections, while [MM](../../people/MM.md) argued that with Composites, Amounts could be composites instead. [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) called the 34-digit limit arbitrary. [WH](../../people/WH.md)'s double-rounding example (0.15 → Decimal → 0.2 vs `(0.15).toPrecision(1)` = "0.1") split the room: [DMM](../../people/DMM.md) called rounding the decimal representation "generally a bug", [WH](../../people/WH.md) held "It is not a bug". A numerics breakout was scheduled for the next day.

The conclusion was that none was reached — the summary records three open uncertainties: rounding edge cases, the equality/Composites relation, and Decimal-vs-Number backing.

## Math.clamp for stage 2

- wiki: [Math.clamp](../../proposals/math-clamp.md)
- proposal: [proposal-math-clamp](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-math-clamp)

[OMT](../../people/OMT.md) (Oliver Medhurst, Invited Expert) presented [Math.clamp](../../proposals/math-clamp.md) for Stage 2 with two open points: placement and `BigInt` support. Placement converged on **`Number.prototype.clamp`** ([MF](../../people/MF.md): the receiver makes value-vs-bounds order obvious; [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) and [WH](../../people/WH.md) agreed), and `BigInt` support was deferred to a separate proposal. [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) complained that materials landed late. The real gate was [WH](../../people/WH.md)'s objection that the spec throws when `min`/`max` are `+0`/`-0` — "I consider throwing when given valid input not to be a minor design feature" — while [JHD](../../people/JHD.md), who favored throwing, was absent.

The conclusion was to continue in a continuation later in the day.

## SeededPRNG for Stage 2

- wiki: [SeededPRNG](../../proposals/seeded-prng.md)
- proposal: [proposal-seeded-random](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-seeded-random)

[TAB](../../people/TAB.md) (Tab Atkins-Bittner, Google) presented [SeededPRNG](../../proposals/seeded-prng.md) for Stage 2: a class seeded from a `Uint8Array` or small integer, with `fromRandomSeed`, `.random()`, `.randomSeed()` for child sequences, and a `state` getter/setter, specified on **ChaCha12** (the Rust/NumPy choice). [MM](../../people/MM.md) raised his quarantine concern about nondeterminism sources, and [KG](../../people/KG.md) noted that a known seed or exposed state enables a communications channel. [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) relayed Mozilla security's doubts (ChaCha12 overkill, risk of crypto misuse — [TAB](../../people/TAB.md): the API is deliberately shaped away from crypto) and asked why it belongs in the language.

The conclusion was Stage 2, with 2.7 reviewers [KG](../../people/KG.md), [JMN](../../people/JMN.md), and [MM](../../people/MM.md) (named the next day) — and the class renamed `Random.Seeded` on the strength of the next topic.

## More Random Functions for Stage 1

- wiki: [More Random Functions](../../proposals/more-random-functions.md)
- proposal: [proposal-random-functions](https://github.com/tc39-transfer/proposal-random-functions/)

[TAB](../../people/TAB.md) followed with a wide-net proposal: a new **`Random` namespace** (`Math.random` stays) covering numbers/ints/BigInts with range and step, shuffles, weighted sampling, non-uniform distributions, random bytes, booleans, and strings, all mirrored on the seeded PRNG. [MF](../../people/MF.md) called it an omnibus to split into 3-4 pieces, with "choose a value within a range" as the strong core. [MM](../../people/MM.md) suggested a class with a global entropy instance, which [KG](../../people/KG.md) countered and [MM](../../people/MM.md) retracted; [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) and [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) favored the single namespace; [DRR](../../people/DRR.md) asked for uniform signatures between namespace functions and instance methods.

The conclusion was Stage 1 for the `Random` namespace with a core of methods (.number/.int/.bigint plus the seeded mirroring), with the array methods and non-uniform distributions split off to new proposals — and all methods also living on `Random.Seeded`.

## Immutable `ArrayBuffer` for Stage 3

- wiki: [Immutable ArrayBuffer](../../proposals/immutable-arraybuffer.md)
- proposal: [proposal-immutable-arraybuffer](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-immutable-arraybuffer)

[RGN](../../people/RGN.md) (Richard Gibson, Agoric) presented the [Immutable ArrayBuffer](../../proposals/immutable-arraybuffer.md) testing plan, with the Stage 3 ask deferred to the next meeting. Edge cases were settled (`sliceToImmutable` follows `slice` for reversed bounds; TypedArray writes fail visibly; DataView, transfer, and Atomics check order), and the plan covers `ArrayBuffer.prototype` methods, the new properties, TypedArray and DataView behavior, and Atomics, to land as a series of small PRs. [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) called it "probably one of the best I've seen"; [MM](../../people/MM.md) noted implementers will not look until Stage 3; [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) noted the testing-plan docs have landed in test262.

The conclusion was that the test262 PRs completing the plan should satisfy Stage 3 at the next meeting.

## Iterator Chunking for Stage 2.7

- wiki: [Iterator Chunking](../../proposals/iterator-chunking.md)
- proposal: [proposal-iterator-chunking](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-iterator-chunking)

[MF](../../people/MF.md) presented [Iterator Chunking](../../proposals/iterator-chunking.md) for Stage 2.7: receivers now close on invalid arguments, matching all iterator helpers. The open question is `.windows()` given fewer items than the window size: (1) yield nothing, (2) throw, (3) yield an undersized window, or (4) pad; other languages mostly choose 1 or 3, and [MF](../../people/MF.md) prefers 1 or 3. [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) noted option 3 is awkward for TypeScript; [DMM](../../people/DMM.md) preferred 3; [MM](../../people/MM.md) doubted `windows` pays for itself; [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) asked for real call sites, which [SFC](../../people/SFC.md), [KG](../../people/KG.md), [LCA](../../people/LCA.md) (Rust usage counts), and [CM](../../people/CM.md) supplied.

The conclusion was that options 1 and 3 are both useful for different use cases — [MF](../../people/MF.md) will split `.windows()` into two methods, settle naming in the issue tracker, and the proposal was not advanced (it stays at Stage 2).

## Continuation: `Math.clamp` for Stage 2

- wiki: [Math.clamp](../../proposals/math-clamp.md)
- proposal: [proposal-math-clamp](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-math-clamp)

[JHD](../../people/JHD.md), now present, spoke to the clamp question. He conceded the `+0`/`-0` case reluctantly — "the way JavaScript treats it per IEEE is confusing and bad… there's no point in continuing that one" — but kept the principle that nonsense inputs should throw, wanting to revisit the `min`/`max` throw within Stage 2 (citing `substring`'s argument-sorting as the bad alternative). [MM](../../people/MM.md) observed that with that settled, clamp has reached Stage 2, and the chairs' check found no opposition.

The conclusion was Stage 2 — as `Number.prototype.clamp`, with `+0` as `min` and `-0` as `max` not throwing, and NaN handling still to be worked out between [WH](../../people/WH.md) and [JHD](../../people/JHD.md).
