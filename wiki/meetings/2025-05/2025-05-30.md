## Comparisons (né Assertions) for Stage 1

- wiki: [Comparisons](../../proposals/comparisons.md)
- proposal: [proposal-comparisons](https://github.com/JakobJingleheimer/proposal-comparisons)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1Nr-9OiZUilFlkUxKd4nHbJf3ie736zx335P_VCXVZ18/view)
- slide: [update](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1lMB6kn7wVR5Nreurg6ykZvNf5uprdJzM2IXP9BX4LvM/view)

[JSH](../../people/JSH.md) (Jacob Smith, OpenJS), presenting his first proposal, asked Stage 1 for Comparisons — two related problems: **A-versus-B** deep comparison (served today by the heavily overlapping `assert`/`expect` of Node, chai, jasmine, and jest, and needed in production for validating aggregate user input) and **failure output** (rendering a usable diff, which userland solves poorly, expensively, and sometimes only with runtime special access). Explicitly out of scope: a test runner or testing utilities. [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) praised the framing, asked whether libraries would adopt a built-in and what happens to the 1% of behavioral differences, and urged outreach to the testing libraries; ZTZ noted getters/Proxy/`toString` want different treatment in testing than in validation; [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) argued testing and validation are better served by two separate APIs (continue-on-fail versus abort, production toggling) — [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) seconded the skepticism about assertions outside tests; [SHS](../../people/SHS.md) added that deeply turning off assertions in dependencies is served by nothing today. [MM](../../people/MM.md) was a "no-go" on piercing proxy encapsulation even through diagnostic output (it could be inspected), skeptical a framework-neutral deep equality is ever good enough for validation, and unwilling to approve without a problem statement distinct from the mechanism — satisfied once [JSH](../../people/JSH.md) stated it (standardized similarity determination, plus usable failure output). [WH](../../people/WH.md) found a "Tower of Babel of terminology" (validation versus invariant checking) and warned that production-stripped asserts must never be doing user-input validation; [ACE](../../people/ACE.md) found the scope far too big — supporting Stage 1 as encouragement ("wind in the sails") on the condition of rapid scope reduction and splitting, with global configuration "there be dragons"; [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) floated a `createRequire`-style factory for customization without global state; [MF](../../people/MF.md) suggested starting from the assertion context with plain SameValue comparisons and expanding equality notions only once the context proves useful.

The conclusion, per the speaker's summary: the proposal was split into three — **Inspector** (detailed information about one or more values, usable standalone or by Comparisons and Pattern Matching), **Modes** (conditionally running code; effectively a `NODE_ENV` replacement that also works in the browser — "especially enticing for its broader applicability"), and **Comparisons** itself (assert/expect; the biggest part, "there be dragons", likely narrow at first with around three methods covering most cases). No stage was granted in this session.

## JS Numerics breakout session

- wiki: [Decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md)
- slide: [link](https://notes.igalia.com/p/tc39-2025-05-decimal-minisummit#/)

[JMN](../../people/JMN.md) ran a decimal mini-summit within the plenary, aiming to clear the path toward a Stage 2 ask. First, the README: [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) said the champions had drifted from the documented use cases and goals, and wanted the long use-cases section made "concise and pointed". Then construction style: `Decimal.from` versus `new Decimal` versus bare `Decimal()` as a coercer — [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) favored following Temporal's `.from` pattern (with [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) doubting the "developers don't like constructors" assumption and [SHS](../../people/SHS.md) noting factory functions remain best practice), while [MM](../../people/MM.md) rejected the premise that Temporal sets the precedent for new APIs, [WH](../../people/WH.md) wanted `Decimal`-as-function coercion semantics, [DMM](../../people/DMM.md) wanted the API to feel light rather than always minting fresh objects, and [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) argued the design must stay future-proofed for decimal primitives. A temperature check split 4 for `Decimal.from` versus 6 unconvinced; a second check on preserving space for future decimal primitives came down 8-9 strong positive to 2 — [EAO](../../people/EAO.md): "a strong indication we ought to continue with the decimal design in a way that preserves the possibility of primitive decimals". The bulk of the session went to String-backed versus Decimal-backed `Amount`: [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) favors a numeric-string black box (a superset of `Number`/`BigInt`/`Decimal` and the Intl-supported ranges), while [WH](../../people/WH.md) opposed the string (exponential-notation edge cases; a value getter that almost never rounds but sometimes does is "a bug farm") and stressed Amount's real value is handing back the **rounded** value for further computation (the tax example: extract the rounded value, add it to the subtotal). [MM](../../people/MM.md) pressed why Amount reifies anything beyond the rendered string, and accepted [SFC](../../people/SFC.md)'s answer (structure, brand checking, parsing, rounding operations, interop, a home for units); it was confirmed that Amounts store the rounded value, the original lost. [WH](../../people/WH.md) and [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) drew opposite conclusions from the same discussion. A Stage 2 temperature check on Decimal (excluding the string-backed Amount): 3 strong positive, 5 positive, 2 confused, 1 indifferent, 1 unconvinced — with [MM](../../people/MM.md) explicitly uncomfortable about Stage 2 if Amount stays attached, but "very happy" for decimal alone.

The conclusion was that the champions commit to discussing a String-backed Amount, and there appears to be good support for Decimal going to Stage 2, though with uncertainty surrounding Amount.

## Revisiting Comparison for Stage 1

- wiki: [Comparisons](../../proposals/comparisons.md)
- proposal: [proposal-comparisons](https://github.com/JakobJingleheimer/proposal-comparisons)
- slide: [update](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1lMB6kn7wVR5Nreurg6ykZvNf5uprdJzM2IXP9BX4LvM/view)

[JSH](../../people/JSH.md) returned with the split proposal and a Stage 1 ask for **Inspector** only: detailed, human-friendly information about one or more values — for example a failed comparison's expected/actual diff — which is expensive to compose in userland, may need special access (proxies, internal slots), and could also serve Pattern Matching, which does not address output. Modes (conditionally running code) needs more problem investigation, and Comparisons proper will be revisited once Inspector and Modes reach Stage 2. [MM](../../people/MM.md) required that **all** special access — not just proxies, internal slots too — live behind Modes, since it breaks the language's encapsulation; with that agreed, he approved. [MF](../../people/MF.md) asked to clarify whether inspecting a value in relation to another is in scope; [WH](../../people/WH.md) supported the investigation on the same condition as [MM](../../people/MM.md); [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) asked whether devtools-style diffs need language-observable internal state or can come from host-internal inspection.

The conclusion was that Inspector reached Stage 1 (special access contingent on the forthcoming Modes proposal), with support from JKP and [WH](../../people/WH.md) and [MM](../../people/MM.md)'s qualified approval — the Comparisons proposal itself remains at Stage 0.
