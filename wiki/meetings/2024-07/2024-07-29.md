## Intl.DurationFormat: Display negative sign on leading numeric-style zeroes

- wiki: [Intl.DurationFormat](../../proposals/intl-durationformat.md)

[BAN](../../people/BAN.md) presented an ECMA-402 fix for [Intl.DurationFormat](../../proposals/intl-durationformat.md) dropping the minus sign when a negative duration starts with zero-filled numeric-style fields (e.g. `new Intl.DurationFormat('en', {hours: 'numeric'}).format({minutes: -1, seconds: -2})` rendered as if positive). The PR ensures the negative sign is displayed on the leading zero.

The conclusion was consensus on the PR.

## Normative Conventions: pretend primitives aren't iterable

- proposal: [how-we-work#152](https://github.com/tc39/how-we-work/pull/152)

[MF](../../people/MF.md) presented the how-we-work normative convention that new generic APIs should treat primitive values (strings, numbers, etc.) as if they were not iterable, avoiding the web-compat hazard of adding methods to primitives and the cross-realm/monkey-patching issues of iterating them. Discussion covered how to word the expectation and what counts as a violation.

The conclusion was consensus on the convention, with all existing violations documented alongside it; [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) to review.

## Normative: Make DefaultNumberOption in ECMA-402 truncate options before validating range

- proposal: [ecma402#908](https://github.com/tc39/ecma402/pull/908)
- slide: [link](https://notes.igalia.com/p/range-checking-inconsistencies#/)

[BAN](../../people/BAN.md) presented the inconsistency between ECMA-402's `DefaultNumberOption` (truncates before range validation) and ECMA-262 conventions (validate then coerce), which produces different errors for non-integral values. A long debate followed over whether validate-then-coerce or coerce-then-validate is right, whether truncation should silently accept `1.5`, and which spec should change.

The conclusion was that ECMA-262 stays unchanged; TG2 will investigate the two orderings and whether to reject non-integral values, and future proposals are encouraged to reject non-integral values rather than truncate.

## Drop assert from import attributes

- wiki: [Import Attributes](../../proposals/import-attributes.md)
- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-import-attributes)
- slide: [proposal-import-attributes#161](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-import-attributes/pull/161)

[NRO](../../people/NRO.md) reported that Chrome and Node have both successfully unshipped the `assert` syntax, so the fallback risk feared when [import attributes](../../proposals/import-attributes.md) shipped is gone. The committee agreed to remove `assert` from the spec, keeping only `with`.

The conclusion was consensus to drop `assert`, with Stage 4 for import attributes planned for the next plenary.

## Concurrency Control

- wiki: [Concurrency Control](../../proposals/concurrency-control.md)
- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/michaelficarra/proposal-concurrency-control)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1rLIzouj1zTr4KdjNrYMZt-FbvEGMPmVeJ8HjOtB6wOU)

[MF](../../people/MF.md) and [LCA](../../people/LCA.md) presented [concurrency control](../../proposals/concurrency-control.md): a governor protocol plus a built-in `Semaphore` and integration with async iterators, motivated by limiting concurrent access to shared resources without blocking the agent. Concerns included the `Semaphore` naming ([SYG](../../people/SYG.md) floated `CountingGovernor`), [MM](../../people/MM.md) on cross-agent sharing and blocking, skepticism from Igalia and Mozilla about the Governor abstraction's complexity, and deadlock semantics ([WH](../../people/WH.md), [KG](../../people/KG.md)).

The conclusion was Stage 1.
