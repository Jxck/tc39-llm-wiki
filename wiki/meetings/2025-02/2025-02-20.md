## Decision Making through Consensus - take 2

- slide: [link](https://github.com/msaboff/tc39/blob/master/TC39%20Consensus%20take%202.pdf)

[MLS](../../people/MLS.md) (Michael Saboff, Apple) revisited his meta topic from a year ago: the committee's decision making is unanimity in practice — a single member withholding consensus is a veto — while the Ecma bylaws are silent on what consensus even means. His concerns: blocking is used by a small group of long-serving, vocal members; a lone blocker has shut down proposals and driven people away from the committee; and the power asymmetry is unwelcoming to newcomers and quieter personality types. His proposal: withholding consensus requires 5% of delegates, minimum two — on the theory that a good-faith principled blocker should be able to convince at least one other delegate. [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) agreed with every aspect of the problem diagnosis but defended the process (it represents the ecosystem, majorities cannot overrun minorities, "no is temporary and yes is forever" on the web); [RPR](../../people/RPR.md) noted escalations to the chair group happen only every 12-18 months; [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) and [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) argued the change would distribute the social cost of blocking, which today is paid entirely by the lone objector while others silently agree; [JSL](../../people/JSL.md) wanted blocking formalized as a motion needing a second, with chairs as a final backstop; [DE](../../people/DE.md) stressed the chronic ambiguity of whether something was blocked or voluntarily withdrawn. [MM](../../people/MM.md) defended unanimity as a system of social norms rather than rules — lone dissent has saved the committee repeatedly ([WH](../../people/WH.md) on SharedArrayBuffer before Meltdown/Spectre, the ES4 collapse) — and warned that browser makers hold a de facto veto anyway, so formal rules would only weaken the community relative to them. The queue ran out; the topic continued in the afternoon.

The conclusion was to continue the discussion (see the continuation below) — no process change was proposed for consensus.

## Continuation: A unified vision for measure and decimal

- wiki: [Amount](../../proposals/amount.md)
- proposal: [measure](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-measure/), [decimal](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-decimal/)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1050DHlNOzcN-8LqJQ_6z8j-LryXgEqOcLfcVzkhJyEk/edit#slide=id.p)
- wiki: [Decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md)

[SFC](../../people/SFC.md) (Shane Carr, Google) continued the previous day's topic with a renamed type — "[Amount](../../proposals/amount.md)" (from `measure.Amount`), following [EAO](../../people/EAO.md)'s feedback — and a tight scope statement: an opaque type carrying a numeric value, its precision, and a dimension (currency or unit); explicitly no unit conversion; constructor, `toLocaleString`, maybe equality and add/subtract. The motivation: a number-with-a-unit is what developers constantly have, it fixes Intl consistency bugs (plural rules, wrong-currency display), it is a prerequisite for the MessageFormat data model, and it narrows the smart-units proposal. The [decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md)/[amount](../../proposals/amount.md) harmony picture projects a number into [decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md) space and then annotates precision and dimension — the pattern [Temporal](../../proposals/temporal.md) proved. He asked two questions: is [decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md) ready for Stage 2 (it has spec text), and is [Amount](../../proposals/amount.md) ready (it does not yet)? The response was skeptical on [Amount](../../proposals/amount.md): [KG](../../people/KG.md) advocated a protocol-only approach (`NumberFormat` accepting an object with the value, rather than a new type), [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) dismissed it as an "Intl NumberFormat options bag factory" whose interop value alone does not motivate a language addition, [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) seconded and urged lighter-weight alternatives, and [MF](../../people/MF.md) warned against limiting [Amount](../../proposals/amount.md) to decimals (rational values with units, like a third, cannot be stored in a [decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md)). [MM](../../people/MM.md) found [NRO](../../people/NRO.md)'s reading of IEEE [decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md)'s trailing-zeros encoding as significant digits "the first justification for this that I heard that makes sense to me". [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) and [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) were informally added to the champion group of the ([BAN](../../people/BAN.md)-on-leave) proposal.

The conclusion, per the speaker's summary: concerns about [Amount](../../proposals/amount.md)'s motivation if its only use case is Intl; requests to explore a protocol-based approach; an open question on the representation of precision; and no delegate raised concerns about [Decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md)'s motivation.

## Continuation: `Number.isSafeNumeric`

- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/Lxxyx/proposal-number-is-safe-numeric/issues/4)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1Noxi5L0jnikYce1h7X67FnjMUbkBQAcMDNafkM7bF4A/edit)

[LIU](../../people/LIU.md) (ZiJian Liu, Alibaba) returned with a reworked problem statement: `Number.isSafeNumeric` would report whether a numeric string, converted to a JavaScript number, retains its original precision and integrity (unsafe = IEEE-754 rounding modifies the significant digits). The motivation is real production pain: Alibaba's MTOP API gateway (100,000+ APIs, over a billion calls a day) must carry Java `Long` values as strings, and DingTalk Sheets ships [decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md).js everywhere because string-to-number conversion loses precision — costing bundle size and first-screen performance. Reception improved but not enough: [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) found the motivation clear at last (and asked whether [Decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md) would obsolete it — [LIU](../../people/LIU.md): no, validation stays useful); [KKL](../../people/KKL.md) (Kris Kowal, Agoric, drawing on Uber's API gateway) sympathized deeply and reframed the space as a family of methods for int64/[decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md) domains rather than a single `Number` method; [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) supported Stage 1; [MF](../../people/MF.md)'s opinion changed on the spot once the round-trip problem statement was articulated. But [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) held his ground — the safety stops at parsing, there is no transitive safety property for later arithmetic, and he did not want the committee to back-form a problem statement the champion is not actually solving — and [GCL](../../people/GCL.md) and [MLS](../../people/MLS.md) (motivation "fairly thin"; Stage 2 bar much higher) also held back.

The conclusion was to withhold consensus for Stage 1, with [CDA](../../people/CDA.md) noting a path forward: nail down a unified problem statement with everyone who participated before the next plenary.

## Language design goal for consensus: Things should layer

- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1Nj6E1h0SeyDGI3e8BQlATQeX-l6x4Jx7uGAM8XimfIM/edit#slide=id.g329dc435965_0_344)

[DE](../../people/DE.md) (Daniel Ehrenberg, Bloomberg) proposed adopting an explicit language design goal: things should layer. Most features should be sugar or libraries on top of core capabilities; a new "capability" should be added only when it is the actual point of the proposal ([Temporal](../../proposals/temporal.md)'s `Now` and time-zone database access are capabilities; most of [Temporal](../../proposals/temporal.md) is sugar; the TDZ check is an ambiguous case; `Map.prototype.getOrInsertComputed` and pattern matching's caching were rejected as accidental capabilities). A key claim: bytecode interpreters and build tools are under similar constraints — neither is magic, neither can do reliable non-local optimization — so syntax should desugar locally and simply. [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) sharpened this: engine limits are externally imposed by loading-performance pressure, unlike AOT tools which can trade compile time for better code; [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) noted tools cannot bail out at runtime; [ACE](../../people/ACE.md) credited tree shaking to the language's static parts, with symbol protocols as the opposite. [KG](../../people/KG.md) pushed back on one point — new capabilities are the best reason to add syntax, so syntax should not be sugar by default — and the statement was refactored: features not motivated by a new capability should be expressible via desugaring, and [composite keys](../../proposals/composite-keys.md), for instance, count as a new capability. [MM](../../people/MM.md) added a preference ordering: more locally desugaring designs are preferred, since desugarability keeps the language's fundamental semantics simple even when implementations never desugar.

The conclusion was conditional consensus on the modified statement: when new features include new capabilities, this should be for a particular reason; the principle is a reference point for future discussions, not a checklist item for stage advancement; wording to be finalized on GitHub with review from [MM](../../people/MM.md), [SYG](../../people/SYG.md), [KG](../../people/KG.md), and [NRO](../../people/NRO.md).

## Continuation: [Decision Making through Consensus - take 2]

[MLS](../../people/MLS.md)'s consensus topic resumed as an extended session. [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) challenged the "fails safe" framing: the current rule fails in ways acceptable to the people in the room, which is survivorship bias — the good ideas killed by lone vetoes are unknowable. [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) and [JSL](../../people/JSL.md) raised that two blockers should be from different organizations, complicated by undisclosed financial relationships between member companies ([REK](../../people/REK.md) cautioned against encoding "financial relationship" definitions in rules at all). [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) drew the symmetry with the earlier change requiring active supporters for advancement, and with [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) preferred a flat "two" over the 5% rule ([MLS](../../people/MLS.md): can live with that). [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) offered the sharpest framing: TC39's blocking culture produces "we can live with it" stage advancements with no active implementer interest — [decorators](../../proposals/decorators.md), [ShadowRealm](../../proposals/shadowrealm.md) — and he would like stage advancement to mean active interest. [JSC](../../people/JSC.md) floated an anonymous TCQ temperature check ("would you block this?") to lower the cost of being the first blocker; [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) and [JGT](../../people/JGT.md) were wary of anonymity, [JSL](../../people/JSL.md) accepting only the temperature check, not anonymous blocks. The pivotal moment was [MM](../../people/MM.md) recounting a hallway conversation with [RPR](../../people/RPR.md): any rule can be gamed, and each escalation of rules weakens social norms — so he moved toward [MLS](../../people/MLS.md) with two concessions: openness to weakening the single veto between Stage 3 and 4 (where investment is massive, and where the class-fields precedent already restricts blocking to implementation reasons), and replacing the second-blocker requirement with a requirement that one other member agree the objection is held in good faith (that member may still support the proposal). [CM](../../people/CM.md) and [AKR](../../people/AKR.md) expressed nervousness about turning two decades of a reasonably working process upside down; [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) argued good faith plus accessibility mitigates most of the social cost; [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) and [DE](../../people/DE.md) countered that expecting superhuman persistence is itself a failure mode with real opportunity costs.

The conclusion, per [RPR](../../people/RPR.md)'s chair summary: the committee broadly agrees there are problems to solve, any cure must not be worse than the disease, and the chairs are open to ideas in plenary and outside — no rule change was made.
