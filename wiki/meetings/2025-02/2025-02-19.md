## A unified vision for measure and decimal

- wiki: [Amount](../../proposals/amount.md)
- proposal: [measure](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-measure/), [decimal](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-decimal/)
- slide: [link](https://notes.igalia.com/p/tc39-2025-02-plenary-decimal-measure-unity)
- wiki: [Decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md)

[JMN](../../people/JMN.md) (Jesse Alama, Igalia) and [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) (Eemeli Aro, Mozilla) explored whether the [decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md) and measure proposals should be merged, given their conceptual overlap, and asked the committee for procedural guidance. The champions argued the whole is greater than the parts — a unified solution for numerics with an easy on-ramp to localization, in the way [Temporal](../../proposals/temporal.md) did for dates — and [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) defended keeping them together; [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) also floated a concrete next step of an opaque "[Amount](../../proposals/amount.md)" type. But the committee pushed back: [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) reported Mozilla's internal discussions were skeptical of merging (and especially of unit conversion), [MF](../../people/MF.md) wanted each part individually justified so a strong part does not carry a weaker one, and [WH](../../people/WH.md) argued arithmetic on decimals is a different concern from units, so the proposals should stay separate while coordinating.

The conclusion was that there is little to no support for merging outside the champion group; conversion between units is a secondary/separate concern; and support exists for at least two proposals, possibly three, with concern that separating them might obscure the value of the sum.

## Stable Formatting update

- wiki: [Stable Formatting](../../proposals/stable-formatting.md)
- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-stable-formatting)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/14KQA1Gyy0reIyouHtzp5ofYRrcwRjkY6GajeknLWhg0/edit?usp=sharing)

[EAO](../../people/EAO.md) (Eemeli Aro, Mozilla) reported progress on [stable formatting](../../proposals/stable-formatting.md): support formed for introducing a `zxx` locale for stable behavior, with the choice of alias still open — `null` was discussed (with concerns about its closeness to `undefined`, which behaves differently in this API), alongside alternatives such as a well-known symbol (`Intl.Stable`) or a longer string identifier. [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) kept the discussion precise: changes to character classification coming with new Unicode versions are just part of Unicode progression, but a revision of the UAX#29 rules themselves should be snapshotted and committed for all time. The non-stable root-locale behavior of `Intl.Collator`, `Intl.Segmenter`, and `toLocale{Lower,Upper}case` was kept distinct from stability — those APIs consume localized data, and giving them a way to access the root locale (e.g. via `und` or `zxx` under a different meaning) needs further work in TG2. [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) noted that an environment needing determinism would simply exclude the unstable APIs.

The conclusion was that [PR #18](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-stable-formatting/pull/18) can be merged, the alias for `zxx` needs further consideration, and if `zxx` explicitly means "stable", another special locale identifier may be needed for the root locale.

## `Error.captureStackTrace()` for Stage 1

- wiki: [Error.captureStackTrace](../../proposals/error-capture-stack-trace.md)
- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/mgaudet/proposal-error-capturestacktrace)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1SFdS9n5JR7Jqz29s7ApvkqDOqOfPW-IaBR2orK828As/edit?usp=sharing)

[MAG](../../people/MAG.md) (Matthew Gaudet, Mozilla) asked for Stage 1 to standardize `Error.captureStackTrace()`, a Chrome-only API since at least 2015 that JSC/WebKit shipped in August 2023 — with SpiderMonkey now implementing it to avoid a web interoperability split, which makes three engines and hence a spec. The method attaches the current stack to any object, with an optional `constructor` argument to elide frames above it. Implementations diverge: V8 installs a getter property, JSC defines a string-valued data property (SpiderMonkey is following JSC). [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) confirmed it does not touch internal slots, and [DE](../../people/DE.md) and [MM](../../people/MM.md) supported Stage 1.

The conclusion was Stage 1, with the summary noting most people would strongly prefer that it produces a data property and does not interfere with the `[[ErrorData]]` internal slot.

## Discussion about shipping non-standard features

[CDA](../../people/CDA.md) reopened a stifled discussion about implementers shipping non-standard features and notifying the committee. [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) proposed no process change, just a courtesy: drop a line on Reflector or Matrix before shipping non-standard stuff, since people here care. [DE](../../people/DE.md) called TypeScript's practice of explaining upcoming features to the committee a good model, stressing it must not become a time to object, and [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) reported V8 plans nothing new with observable behavior (parse hints, currently in origin trial, being the interesting exception), pointing to the Chrome and Mozilla release notes as the standing channel; [MLS](../../people/MLS.md) said Apple rarely ships non-standard features.

The conclusion was only a discussion: the committee prefers early notification (before or after shipping) of non-standard work, with no process change made.

## Error Stack Accessor

- wiki: [Error Stack Accessor](../../proposals/error-stack-accessor.md)
- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/ljharb/proposal-error-stack-accessor)

[JHD](../../people/JHD.md) (Jordan Harband) split his decade-old error stack proposal in two and brought the piece that only standardizes what browsers already do: an accessor property `Error.prototype.stack` whose getter throws on non-objects, returns `undefined` off error objects for web compatibility, tolerates implementation-defined behavior, and whose setter throws on non-objects and otherwise sets a shadowing own data property — so borrowing the getter still reaches the original stack. [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) reported initial Firefox nightly telemetry as positive for web compatibility, [KG](../../people/KG.md) flagged that `DOMException`s (real errors now, with `[[ErrorData]]`) only sometimes have stacks and that `new DOMException` in Chrome would gain one, and [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) asked to work out the DOMException interaction in an HTML PR.

The conclusion was Stage 2, with [MF](../../people/MF.md) and [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) as spec reviewers, and the requirement that an HTML integration PR be directionally approved (and possibly merged) before Stage 2.7.

## Stabilize integrity traits status update

- wiki: [Stabilize](../../proposals/stabilize.md)
- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-stabilize)
- slide: [link](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-stabilize/blob/main/stabilize-talks/stabilize-stage1-status-update.pdf)

[MM](../../people/MM.md) (Mark Miller, Agoric) updated the committee on unbundling integrity traits. The committee agreed not to unbundle non-extensible (accepting a loss of virtualizability in a corner case), and hopes to bundle the "fixed" trait into non-extensible — Google has usage counters running to decide it. On the override mistake, the hope is to fix it globally with narrow carve-outs for the legacy lodash case (via [JRL](../../people/JRL.md)'s `toString`-only pattern), which again only a major browser can measure; the counters may only capture strict mode, in which case the fix could be strict-only. [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) probed edge cases (a stabilized pending promise, RegExp and array built-ins that write `lastIndex`/length), which [MM](../../people/MM.md) took under advisement, noting "fixed" alone does not guarantee safety from re-entrancy without an is-record-like predicate.

The conclusion was that if all this works out, only the "non-trapping" trait remains, becoming the root trait — possibly renamed from "[stabilize](../../proposals/stabilize.md)" back to "fixed" (its original name circa 2010) — addressing a major source of proxy re-entrancy hazards without threatening proxy transparency.

## Records and Tuples future directions

- wiki: [Records & Tuples](../../proposals/records-and-tuples.md)
- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-record-tuple)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1uONn7T91lfZDV4frCsxpwd1QB_pU3P7F6V2j9jEPnA8/)

[ACE](../../people/ACE.md) (Ashley Claymore, Bloomberg) reopened [Records & Tuples](../../proposals/records-and-tuples.md) (nominally Stage 2, dormant for about a year) not to advance it but to explore a redesign. Recapping the constraints — no new primitives, and implementer feedback that neither interning (too costly) nor deep-comparison `===` (must stay a pointer comparison) works — [DE](../../people/DE.md) framed the new direction: syntax (possibly optional), shallow immutability, objects that are composite-key comparable from birth, so existing `Map`/`Set` semantics could treat them structurally equal without changing `===`. [KG](../../people/KG.md) was positive — [composite keys](../../proposals/composite-keys.md) working transparently with `groupBy`, `Map`, and `Set` is "the main thing that I personally want out of [records and tuples](../../proposals/records-and-tuples.md)" — with [MF](../../people/MF.md) agreeing. Discussion ranged widely: [JGT](../../people/JGT.md) and [JRL](../../people/JRL.md) on React (props [comparisons](../../proposals/comparisons.md) versus the VDOM tree, where deep equality would turn linear into quadratic; the React compiler has reduced the use case), [WH](../../people/WH.md) on how a modified SameValueZero would treat non-records, [LCA](../../people/LCA.md) hoping [Temporal](../../proposals/temporal.md) types could one day opt into [composite keys](../../proposals/composite-keys.md) ([DE](../../people/DE.md): ship [Temporal](../../proposals/temporal.md) as-is), and [MAH](../../people/MAH.md) worrying how old code holding objects by reference behaves when new code introduces these objects, plus whether custom prototypes could participate ([KG](../../people/KG.md): tricky; it depends on whether the main value is [composite keys](../../proposals/composite-keys.md) or general-purpose immutable objects).

The conclusion was feedback generally positive to continue exploring this direction, with noted concern about complexity when existing code expects objects in a `Map` to use reference equality only.

## Use cases for ShadowRealm

- wiki: [ShadowRealm](../../proposals/shadowrealm.md)
- proposal: [proposal](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-shadowrealm)

[PFC](../../people/PFC.md) (Philip Chimento, Igalia) followed up the previous day's [ShadowRealm](../../proposals/shadowrealm.md) status update with a meta-point: TC39 and the web platform mean different things by "use cases". Quoting the W3C TAG's explainer-writing guide, he argued web platform reviewers want the problem framed from the end user's perspective — "this feature allows developers to do X" does not count — and rewrote the [ShadowRealm](../../proposals/shadowrealm.md) use case accordingly (plugin isolation delivering a stable experience to platform users). [MM](../../people/MM.md) added supply-chain risk as an explicit, vividly end-user-framed use case (97% of a typical application is third-party code). [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) was blunt about where the decision actually lives: the bar is in WHATWG, active browser buy-in on the non-JS-engine side, and asking the room's engine representatives does not progress it. [KG](../../people/KG.md) and [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) clarified that the requirement to integrate host APIs comes from inside the committee — "I don't want this feature to exist if it doesn't have TextEncoder" — leading [MAH](../../people/MAH.md) to the rueful "So we brought this on ourselves?". [JSL](../../people/JSL.md) advised getting agreement on the problem statement from WHATWG before presenting use cases, and noted WinterTC APIs will face the same issue.

The conclusion was a shared understanding rather than a decision: [ShadowRealm](../../proposals/shadowrealm.md)'s champions need to frame use cases in end-user terms for the web platform, and the Stage 3 blocker is web platform integration agreed in advance.
