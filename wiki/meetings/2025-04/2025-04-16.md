## Intl Era Month Code Stage 2 Update

- wiki: [Intl Era Month Code](../../proposals/intl-era-month-code.md)
- proposal: [proposal-intl-era-monthcode](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1wvJoRFa8nRjlYSHuVLpxx-wCfwt4H9NIw2fsGJ72gxs/edit#slide=id.p)

[SFC](../../people/SFC.md) (Shane Carr, Google) updated on [Intl Era Month Code](../../proposals/intl-era-month-code.md). Era codes were renamed from a BCP47-based globally-unique scheme to actual era names/acronyms — losing global uniqueness ("am" means different things in the Coptic, Ethiopian, Hebrew, and ROC calendars — but gaining familiarity. On arithmetical calendars, the Chinese and Dangie calendars default to a layered ISO year, matching the polyfill. The hardest problem is Hijri: the calendars are observation-based, so simulations cannot predict month starts (the Eid split example) and round-tripping is unreliable; the draft proposal is to ship three grounded variants — the Saudi Umm al-Qura almanac plus two tabular algorithms — with `"islamic"` resolving in `Intl.DateTimeFormat` to e.g. umalqura via the existing conformant fallback, and the constraint that what Intl resolves, [Temporal](../../proposals/temporal.md) resolves. CLDR is likely to adopt the recommendations. Issue [#2869](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/issues/2869) (distant dates — the Chinese calendar 25,000 years out) split the room into approximate/best-effort and throw camps; [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) prefers approximation plus a possible follow-on "safe date" proposal. [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) reported that ICU4C and ICU4X simulations disagree and produce impossible dates, web usage is small (telemetry planned), and volunteered as an additional 2.7 reviewer alongside [KHG](../../people/KHG.md).

The conclusion was that an update was given, with [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) hoping to ask for Stage 2.7 later this year.

## Compare Strings by Codepoint for stage 1 or 2

- wiki: [Compare Strings by Codepoint](../../proposals/compare-strings-by-codepoint.md)
- proposal: [proposal-compare-strings-by-codepoint](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-compare-strings-by-codepoint)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1eTuB1jjgb2_xG_zMNmkhleJx1F0QviMEwkkBUL9ezPQ/)

[MAH](../../people/MAH.md) (Mathieu Hofman, Agoric) proposed `String.codePointCompare`, a comparator matching UTF-8/code-point order. JavaScript's default UTF-16 code-unit comparison sorts surrogate-encoded characters (U+10000 and above) below the U+E000-FFFF range, and locale comparators are unstable and human-oriented (confusables, equivalence classes) — neither is a portable total order. Other languages and databases (Swift, Go, Rust, SQLite) already use code-point order, and Agoric needs it in earnest: collections with a heap-Map and an SQLite backing store must iterate identically. [KG](../../people/KG.md) was supportive, also wanting comparator coherence resolved before [Stable Formatting](../../proposals/stable-formatting.md) reaches 2.7; [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) noted UTF-16 order is a valid total order too and asked about userland-shim-versus-builtin and benchmarks; [ABO](../../people/ABO.md) observed that even encoding experts miss this (the Nova engine's WTF-8 bug); [WH](../../people/WH.md) said it "should have been done long ago" — surrogates were added to UTF-16 late; [MF](../../people/MF.md) asked to explore generalized iterable comparators (issue #6), which [MAH](../../people/MAH.md) noted are hard to optimize.

The conclusion was Stage 1.

## Update to Consensus policy

- slide: [link](https://github.com/msaboff/tc39/blob/master/TC39%20Consensus%20Apr%202025.pdf)

[MLS](../../people/MLS.md) (Michael Saboff, Apple) continued his consensus-policy topic from Seattle with a concrete proposal: withholding consensus would require a second delegate who either also withholds or voices support, and who is not from the same ECMA member. [SHN](../../people/SHN.md) (Ecma) noted the bylaws only recognize members as voting; [WH](../../people/WH.md) was uncomfortable creating second-class invited experts — "That seems wrong" — if they may support but not oppose. [DE](../../people/DE.md) proposed a cooling-off procedure instead (a block lasts one meeting with recorded reasons, overridable at the next). [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) opposed qualifying the second by financial relationships (Mozilla-Google, Apple-Google — the three-implementer reality) and insisted late-agenda blocking must remain possible; [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) preferred good-faith wording over a blanket ban. [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) asked what flexibility TC39 even has under the Ecma bylaws (SHN: no clear answer today). [MM](../../people/MM.md) objected to the whole framing — it recasts the second as someone attesting the blocker's good faith, and every bad-faith block he has experienced came from a browser maker; [WH](../../people/WH.md) added that the common case for blocking is bugs and not-readiness — "This asks people to block based on things they don't understand". [MM](../../people/MM.md) was unwilling to accept even the good-faith-supporter version ("fixing a non-problem… worse than the disease"), while [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) and [MM](../../people/MM.md) disagreed on the politics: [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) sees a single veto as concentrating procedural power; [MM](../../people/MM.md) cited the WHATWG precedent that browsers deciding among themselves should be publicly costly.

The conclusion was that some were in favor and some thought it worse than the status quo; the discussion continues at future plenaries.

## Stage 1 update for decimal & measure: Amounts

- wiki: [Amount](../../proposals/amount.md)
- proposal: [proposal-decimal](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-decimal)
- slide: [link](https://notes.igalia.com/p/tc39-2025-04-decimal-intl-integration#/)
- wiki: [Decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md)

[JMN](../../people/JMN.md) (Jesse Alama, Igalia), presenting with [BAN](../../people/BAN.md) on sick leave, updated the [Amount](../../proposals/amount.md) design: a placeholder `Decimal.Something` (maybe `Decimal.Amount` or `Decimal.WithPrecision`) — a thin class wrapping a [decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md) with precision information for Intl's benefit, which measure may later extend with units; [decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md) and measure remain separate proposals. Construction is via `Decimal.prototype` convenience methods; the object carries significant/fractional-digit precision ([WH](../../people/WH.md) caught that 42.56 at 2 significant digits must round to 43), exposes an accessor and `toString`, does no arithmetic, and is immutable. The champions are thinking of banning bare decimals in `PluralRules` (and possibly `NumberFormat`) in favor of Amounts. [WH](../../people/WH.md) pressed hard: this is now three classes; precision is not [decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md)-specific; bare Numbers would be allowed while bare Decimals throw; the split breaks commutativity of setting units and precision; and IEEE's quantum cannot represent "N digits after the point" (denormals) — store precision separately instead. [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) defended making the safe thing easy — bare numbers cause locale-dependent precision-mismatch bugs — while [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) disagreed with banning bare decimals (they are formattable finite values). [MM](../../people/MM.md) found the two-class idea attractive but nothing [decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md)-specific — it should apply to Number/BigInt too (his crypto/Satoshi example he later withdrew as merely illustrative) — and called IEEE-quantum-as-significant-digits a "semantics violating pun", which [NRO](../../people/NRO.md), [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) (cohort and 1-34 significant digits are equivalent, modulo subnormals), and [DE](../../people/DE.md) treated as a defensible judgment call. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) added that a polymorphic [amount](../../proposals/amount.md) means branching and discriminants, while a [decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md)-backed [amount](../../proposals/amount.md) gets precision for free and interoperates with Decimal128 platforms.

The conclusion, per the speaker's summary: some concern about the commutativity of applying a unit and a precision; discussion of Decimal128's representation of very large/precise numbers (cryptocurrency); and some concerns about the proposed "ban" of bare [decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md) values in Intl.

## Guidelines for Locale-Sensitive Testing in Test262

- slide: [link](https://ptomato.name/talks/tc39-2025-04/#8)

[PFC](../../people/PFC.md) (Philip Chimento, Igalia) presented (not normative) on testing ILD — implementation- and locale-defined — behavior in Test262: the ECMA-402 spec permits almost any string for formatted output, and requiring golden output would freeze culture in time, while a "mini-implementation" (the old testIntl.js polyfill) duplicates the spec. Alternatives explored: asserting stable substrings, comparative testing (fails: numeric day vs 2-digit day for values over 9), and metamorphic testing ([RGN](../../people/RGN.md): `dateStyle: 'full'` output must contain the day and long month name that `dateStyle`'s parts would produce). [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) gave the war stories: an en-US thin-space change broke real sites, and sv-SE copypaste formats; [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) framed it as risk management — en-US stability is the highest priority; [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) argued spec-shaped metamorphic tests are safe (DurationFormat is NumberFormat plus ListFormat) and that en-US goldens are not a great solution.

The conclusion was to continue the work on tc39/test262#3786, with en-US (and sv-SE) potentially needing higher stability requirements.

## `export defer` extracted from `import defer`: stage 2 update or for stage 1

- wiki: [import defer](../../proposals/import-defer.md)
- proposal: [proposal-deferred-reexports](https://github.com/nicolo-ribaudo/proposal-deferred-reexports)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1ats5CbsgalobhnfFIR2b1QAdaLRe4yVI55meo_ARqdU)

[NRO](../../people/NRO.md) (Nicolò Ribaudo, Igalia) presented `export defer`, the piece of the modules work that stayed behind when `import defer` split off (the latter reached Stage 3 in February). Barrel files (re-export everything) cause unnecessary loading and execution; tree shaking is unreliable (side effects; webpack/Rollup/Parcel differences) and CJS getters do not map onto ESM. `export defer` marks re-exports so the underlying modules load and evaluate only when a binding is actually accessed — stronger than `import defer`, which defers evaluation but not loading — and it composes with `import *`, isolating each name. [WH](../../people/WH.md) noted async dependencies of deferred modules still execute. [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) was skeptical: it is still worse than importing directly, and tree shaking is "currently and always a sub par solution"; [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) worried the ESM feature matrix keeps growing (top-level await, `import defer`, `export defer`) and asked about a side-effects-annotation alternative, which [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) answered with: the keyword carries semantics you cannot otherwise represent; [ACE](../../people/ACE.md) mused this should have been export's default semantics. Support came from [MM](../../people/MM.md), [DLM](../../people/DLM.md), [JWK](../../people/JWK.md), and [DJM](../../people/DJM.md).

The conclusion was that `export defer` is reaffirmed at Stage 2 (continuing its status from when [import defer](../../proposals/import-defer.md) split off).
