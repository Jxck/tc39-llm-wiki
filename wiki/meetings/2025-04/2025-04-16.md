## Intl Era Month Code Stage 2 Update

- wiki: [Intl Era Month Code](../../proposals/intl-era-month-code.md)
- proposal: [proposal-intl-era-monthcode](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1wvJoRFa8nRjlYSHuVLpxx-wCfwt4H9NIw2fsGJ72gxs/edit#slide=id.p)

[SFC](../../people/SFC.md) (Shane Carr, Google) updated on [Intl Era Month Code](../../proposals/intl-era-month-code.md). Era codes were renamed from globally-unique BCP47-based identifiers to familiar era names, losing uniqueness ("am" means different things in several calendars). The Chinese and Dangi calendars default to a layered ISO year. The hardest problem is Hijri: observation-based calendars cannot be predicted, so the draft ships three grounded variants (Umm al-Qura plus two tabular algorithms), with `"islamic"` resolving to one of them and the rule that what Intl resolves, [Temporal](../../proposals/temporal.md) resolves. Issue #2869 (distant dates) split the room between best-effort approximation and throwing. [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) reported ICU4C and ICU4X simulations disagree and volunteered as a 2.7 reviewer alongside [KHG](../../people/KHG.md).

The conclusion was that an update was given, with [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) hoping to ask for Stage 2.7 later this year.

## Compare Strings by Codepoint for stage 1 or 2

- wiki: [Compare Strings by Codepoint](../../proposals/compare-strings-by-codepoint.md)
- proposal: [proposal-compare-strings-by-codepoint](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-compare-strings-by-codepoint)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1eTuB1jjgb2_xG_zMNmkhleJx1F0QviMEwkkBUL9ezPQ/)

[MAH](../../people/MAH.md) (Mathieu Hofman, Agoric) proposed `String.codePointCompare`, a comparator matching UTF-8/code-point order. The default UTF-16 code-unit order sorts supplementary characters below U+E000-FFFF, and locale comparators are unstable, so neither gives a portable total order; Swift, Go, Rust, and SQLite use code-point order, and Agoric needs heap and SQLite-backed collections to iterate identically. [KG](../../people/KG.md) supported and wanted comparator coherence settled before [Stable Formatting](../../proposals/stable-formatting.md) reaches 2.7; [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) asked about userland shims and benchmarks; [WH](../../people/WH.md) said it "should have been done long ago"; [MF](../../people/MF.md) asked to explore generalized iterable comparators (issue #6).

The conclusion was Stage 1.

## Update to Consensus policy

- slide: [link](https://github.com/msaboff/tc39/blob/master/TC39%20Consensus%20Apr%202025.pdf)

[MLS](../../people/MLS.md) (Michael Saboff, Apple) continued his consensus-policy topic with a concrete proposal: withholding consensus would require a second delegate, not from the same Ecma member, who also withholds or voices support. SHN noted the bylaws only recognize members as voting; [WH](../../people/WH.md) objected to making invited experts second-class; [DE](../../people/DE.md) proposed a one-meeting cooling-off procedure instead; [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) opposed qualifying the second by financial relationships. [MM](../../people/MM.md) objected to the framing — every bad-faith block he has seen came from a browser maker — and called it "worse than the disease"; [WH](../../people/WH.md) noted most blocks are about bugs and unreadiness. [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) sees a single veto as concentrating procedural power, while [MM](../../people/MM.md) cited the WHATWG precedent that browsers deciding among themselves should be publicly costly.

The conclusion was that some were in favor and some thought it worse than the status quo; the discussion continues at future plenaries.

## Stage 1 update for decimal & measure: Amounts

- wiki: [Amount](../../proposals/amount.md), [Decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md)
- proposal: [proposal-decimal](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-decimal)
- slide: [link](https://notes.igalia.com/p/tc39-2025-04-decimal-intl-integration#/)

[JMN](../../people/JMN.md) (Jesse Alama, Igalia), presenting with [BAN](../../people/BAN.md) on sick leave, updated the [Amount](../../proposals/amount.md) design: a thin, immutable class (placeholder `Decimal.Amount`/`Decimal.WithPrecision`) wrapping a decimal with precision for Intl, with no arithmetic, which measure may later extend with units; decimal and measure stay separate. The champions considered banning bare decimals in `PluralRules` (maybe `NumberFormat`). [WH](../../people/WH.md) objected that precision is not decimal-specific, bare Numbers would be allowed while bare Decimals throw, and splitting units and precision breaks commutativity. [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) defended making the safe thing easy; [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) opposed the ban; [MM](../../people/MM.md) wanted it to apply to Number/BigInt too and called IEEE-quantum-as-precision a "semantics violating pun", which [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) and [DE](../../people/DE.md) considered defensible.

The conclusion, per the speaker's summary: some concern about the commutativity of applying a unit and a precision; discussion of Decimal128's representation of very large/precise numbers (cryptocurrency); and some concerns about the proposed "ban" of bare decimal values in Intl.

## Guidelines for Locale-Sensitive Testing in Test262

- slide: [link](https://ptomato.name/talks/tc39-2025-04/#8)

[PFC](../../people/PFC.md) (Philip Chimento, Igalia) presented (not normative) on testing implementation- and locale-defined behavior in Test262: ECMA-402 permits almost any formatted output, golden outputs would freeze culture in time, and a mini-implementation duplicates the spec. Alternatives were stable substrings, comparative testing, and metamorphic testing (e.g. [RGN](../../people/RGN.md)'s check that `dateStyle: 'full'` contains the expected parts). [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) recalled an en-US thin-space change breaking real sites; [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) framed it as risk management with en-US stability the top priority; [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) favored spec-shaped metamorphic tests over en-US goldens.

The conclusion was to continue the work on tc39/test262#3786, with en-US (and sv-SE) potentially needing higher stability requirements.

## `export defer` extracted from `import defer`: stage 2 update or for stage 1

- wiki: [import defer](../../proposals/import-defer.md)
- proposal: [proposal-deferred-reexports](https://github.com/nicolo-ribaudo/proposal-deferred-reexports)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1ats5CbsgalobhnfFIR2b1QAdaLRe4yVI55meo_ARqdU)

[NRO](../../people/NRO.md) (Nicolò Ribaudo, Igalia) presented `export defer`, left behind when [import defer](../../proposals/import-defer.md) split off (the latter reached Stage 3 in February). Barrel files cause unnecessary loading and execution and tree shaking is unreliable; `export defer` marks re-exports so the underlying modules load and evaluate only when a binding is accessed, going further than `import defer`, which defers evaluation but not loading. [WH](../../people/WH.md) noted async dependencies still execute; [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) was skeptical that it beats importing directly; [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) worried about the growing ESM feature matrix and asked about a side-effects annotation instead; [ACE](../../people/ACE.md) mused it should have been export's default. Support came from [MM](../../people/MM.md), [DLM](../../people/DLM.md), [JWK](../../people/JWK.md), and [DJM](../../people/DJM.md).

The conclusion was that `export defer` is reaffirmed at Stage 2 (continuing its status from when import defer split off).
