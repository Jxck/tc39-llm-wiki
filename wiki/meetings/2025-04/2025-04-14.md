## Normative: add notation to PluralRules

- proposal: [ECMA-402 PR #989](https://github.com/tc39/ecma402/pull/989)

[USA](../../people/USA.md) (Ujjwal Sharma, Igalia) presented a small normative PR adding a `notation` option to `Intl.PluralRules`. When Unified NumberFormat added notations (scientific, compact, etc.) to `NumberFormat` in 2018, the matching PluralRules support slipped through the cracks, although plural categories can depend on it (e.g. Spanish compact millions). The PR threads the notation through the options AO shared with `NumberFormat`, and TG2 approved it in April 2025. [DE](../../people/DE.md) suggested tracking it like a staged proposal until implementations and tests exist, which [USA](../../people/USA.md) noted the ECMA-402 progress-tracking wiki already does.

The conclusion was consensus on the PR, with explicit support from [DE](../../people/DE.md) and [DLM](../../people/DLM.md).

## Normative: Mark sync module evaluation promise as handled

- proposal: [spec PR #3535](https://github.com/tc39/ecma262/pull/3535)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1kheOg1AZDj-T9n0O-0sbd5IBwvnjiODyJcK1Ez6Q0JU)

[NRO](../../people/NRO.md) (Nicolò Ribaudo, Igalia) presented a fix for module evaluation rejections and the `HostPromiseRejectionTracker` hook: non-cyclic Module Records return an already-settled promise whose rejection the machinery extracts by reading internal slots, so hosts never learn it was handled and fire a spurious `unhandledRejection`. The fix calls the host hook when the rejection is extracted (an AO shared with [import defer](../../proposals/import-defer.md)); it is unobservable on the web, and Node.js already behaves this way. [MM](../../people/MM.md), relaying [MAH](../../people/MAH.md)'s concern from the PR, objected that spec-internal promises become observable to hosts, while [KG](../../people/KG.md) argued the change makes them less observable in practice.

The conclusion was that the PR was blocked: [MM](../../people/MM.md) withheld consensus pending [MAH](../../people/MAH.md), who could not attend that day, despite explicit support from [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) and others — it reached consensus in a continuation on Day 4.

## Note about changed behavior of `Array.fromAsync` after landing #2600

- wiki: [Array.fromAsync](../../proposals/array-from-async.md)
- proposal: [issue #39](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-array-from-async/issues/39#issuecomment-1526744932)

[KG](../../people/KG.md) (Kevin Gibbons, F5) gave a heads-up that landing [ecma262 PR #2600](https://github.com/tc39/ecma262/pull/2600/) changed the behavior of `Array.fromAsync` without any change to its spec text. The PR fixed the `for await` machinery so that when a synchronous iterator yields a promise that rejects, the async-wrapper closes the underlying iterator (a sync iterator cannot clean up the way an async iterable can) — a necessary change for the invariant that iterators are always closed. Since `Array.fromAsync` shares that machinery, its behavior changed too, which was easy to miss because the two live in different places in the spec. Test262 tests were updated in [PR #4450](https://github.com/tc39/test262/pull/4450); [JSC](../../people/JSC.md) noted V8 already passes while other engines do not yet, and that work on `Array.fromAsync` has resumed, hoping to reach Stage 4 within the year.

The conclusion was that this was a notification only — no consensus was requested.

## `AsyncContext` Stage 2 Update

- wiki: [AsyncContext](../../proposals/async-context.md)
- proposal: [proposal-async-context](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-async-context)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1YkSQIWxCQCLSe1WKFWpndEd-gdf9coE1HRYa_v_z5J0/)

[ABO](../../people/ABO.md) (Andreu Botella, Igalia) answered Mozilla's earlier negative feedback: [AsyncContext](../../proposals/async-context.md) is a power feature for library authors and independently valuable to front-end frameworks — React transitions, Solid scopes, Svelte's `getRequestEvent`, and Vue's component context all lose context across `await`. [CZW](../../people/CZW.md) (Bloomberg) and [SHS](../../people/SHS.md) (Google tracing) gave testimonials. Web integration moved from registration-time snapshots (a leak) to a **dispatch context** model with a root-context fallback. [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) found the use cases better stated, but Mozilla still sees a web-integration cost they do not justify; [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) said Chrome remains positive; [DE](../../people/DE.md) urged a generic rule largely inferable from WebIDL.

The conclusion, per the speaker's summary: multiple frontend frameworks are eagerly waiting for AsyncContext, and while the use cases were found convincing, it is still unclear they are worth the web-integration cost — browsers hold opposite opinions on that tradeoff.

## Temporal Update

- wiki: [Temporal](../../proposals/temporal.md)
- proposal: [proposal-temporal](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-temporal)
- slide: [link](https://ptomato.name/talks/tc39-2025-04/#1)

[PFC](../../people/PFC.md) (Philip Chimento, Igalia) reported that [Temporal](../../proposals/temporal.md) is shipping in Firefox nightly, and [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) confirmed Firefox 139 will ship it. Test-conformance keeps rising, with GraalJS and especially Boa making substantial gains. On the long-standing concern about BigInt in the spec's internal computations, [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) showed a proof of concept representing epoch nanoseconds and time durations as a pair of 64-bit floats — no nonstandard integer sizes needed, handling even `Duration.prototype.total`'s extra-precise division correctly. The locale-defined behaviors under coordination with TG2 are open issues in the separate [Intl Era and Month Code](../../proposals/intl-era-month-code.md) proposal ([#32](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/issues/32) where year zero starts in various calendars, [#30](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/issues/30) constraining nonexistent leap months, [#27](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/issues/27) era codes) — CLDR does not yet define them, and the agreement there will flow into the Intl libraries implementations pull in.

The conclusion was that Temporal stays at Stage 3 and is ready to ship.

## Composite Keys for stage 1

- wiki: [Composite Keys](../../proposals/composite-keys.md)
- proposal: [proposal-composites](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-composites)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1n7lj_y02f4QjrTMvRGs3aZXt_zji5nSVYGhmVchMpvI/)

[ACE](../../people/ACE.md) (Ashley Claymore, Bloomberg) proposed "composites", reimagining the [Records & Tuples](../../proposals/records-and-tuples.md) problem: two objects with the same data are two `Set` entries, so developers build `JSON.stringify` keys ([CZW](../../people/CZW.md): OpenTelemetry does exactly that). Composites are frozen-from-birth ordinary objects (no new primitives) whose equality recurses through composites and falls back to SameValueZero, used by `Composite.equals`, `Map`/`Set` keys, and `Array.prototype.includes`/`indexOf` only when the argument is a composite. [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) preferred interning so `===` works, which [ACE](../../people/ACE.md) and [MM](../../people/MM.md) (unregistered symbols make canonicalization impossible) pushed back on; [MF](../../people/MF.md) and [WH](../../people/WH.md) debated SameValue vs SameValueZero; [KG](../../people/KG.md) said it "gives me everything I personally wanted from Records & Tuples".

The conclusion was consensus for Stage 1 (explicit support from [JH](../../people/JH.md), [CDA](../../people/CDA.md), [DLM](../../people/DLM.md), [MF](../../people/MF.md), [CZW](../../people/CZW.md), [NRO](../../people/NRO.md), [MM](../../people/MM.md), [SYG](../../people/SYG.md), and [WH](../../people/WH.md)), with canonicalization and -0 handling to be discussed during Stage 1.

## Withdrawing Records & Tuples

- wiki: [Records & Tuples](../../proposals/records-and-tuples.md)
- proposal: [proposal-record-tuple](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-record-tuple)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1afxyqJthBWsOpBvmPFP-VOhT8KyVF_AQlXLj0nkY6v4/)

[ACE](../../people/ACE.md) returned to formally withdraw [Records & Tuples](../../proposals/records-and-tuples.md): the proposal's core was adding new primitives, which never found a path forward, while the reimagined composites proposal had just reached Stage 1 the same day. [NRO](../../people/NRO.md)'s queue message: "RIP R&T, you'll be missed." [MM](../../people/MM.md) floated composites for Stage 2 on the spot, but [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) pointed out there is no spec text yet. [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) raised naming: "composite" is hard to remember (composite or composable?), and with the "record" space now cleared the name could be reclaimed — or "key" could lean into the use case; [ACE](../../people/ACE.md) was open to anything except "Record", which carries too much TypeScript precedent, and noted the direction question (key-oriented tool vs general data model) should be settled first.

The conclusion was that the Records & Tuples proposal has been withdrawn.
