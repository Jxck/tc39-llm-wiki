## Don't Remember Panicking Stage 1 Update

- wiki: [Don't Remember Panicking](../../proposals/dont-remember-panicking.md)
- proposal: [proposal-oom-fails-fast](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-oom-fails-fast/tree/master)
- slide: [link](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-oom-fails-fast/blob/master/panic-talks/dont-remember-panicking.pdf)

[MM](../../people/MM.md) (Mark Miller, Agoric) updated his Stage 1 proposal, renamed from "OOM Must Fail Fast" to "[Don't Remember Panicking](../../proposals/dont-remember-panicking.md)". The spec does not admit out-of-memory or stack exhaustion, which makes patterns like prepare-commit unimplementable; browsers throw-and-continue while XS fail-stops. The current shape is a `HostFaultHandler` host hook with a fault taxonomy and a user-level `Reflect.panic`, aborting a **dynamic agent cluster** in the spirit of Erlang's let-it-crash. [DE](../../people/DE.md) worried `Reflect.panic` lets libraries halt whole programs; [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) warned panicking assert libraries would harm the web; [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) said the only realistic `Reflect.panic` is a process crash that browsers will not ship; [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) suggested a separate transaction proposal instead.

The conclusion was no conclusion — the topic continued in a continuation the same day, including a temperature check on viability without the panic API.

## Enums for Stage 1

- wiki: [Enums](../../proposals/enums.md)
- proposal: [proposal-enum](https://github.com/rbuckton/proposal-enum)
- slide: [link](https://1drv.ms/p/c/934f1675ed4c1638/EYypvengQohMlG52w1qseW8BCwCkSG0Y-2ip8Zq7pxoOFw?e=Aklyqu)

[RBN](../../people/RBN.md) (Ron Buckton, Microsoft) proposed standardizing enums as the erasable design that Node.js type stripping needs (TypeScript enums are not erasable). Over an object literal, enums offer a closed domain (frozen, null prototype), restricted value types, self-reference for bit flags, and tooling recognition; future directions include ADT enums feeding pattern matching, [decorators](../../proposals/decorators.md), and auto-initializers. Intended divergences from TypeScript: no default auto-numbering, likely no reverse mapping, no `const enum`. [MM](../../people/MM.md) flagged TDZ issues in the desugaring but was enthusiastic ("enums were the biggest hole"); [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) asked how TypeScript would adopt the divergent semantics and for a DX-versus-runtime-cost assessment.

The conclusion was that the proposal advanced to Stage 1, with support from [MM](../../people/MM.md), [PFC](../../people/PFC.md), [JHX](../../people/JHX.md), [CDA](../../people/CDA.md), and [NRO](../../people/NRO.md).

## `Object.propertyCount` for stage 1 or 2

- wiki: [Object.propertyCount](../../proposals/object-propertycount.md)
- proposal: [object-property-count](https://github.com/ljharb/object-property-count)
- slide: [link](https://github.com/tc39/agendas/blob/main/2025/2025.04%20-%20Object.propertyCount%20slides.pdf)

[RBR](../../people/RBR.md) (Ruben Bridgewater, Invited Expert), with co-champion [JHD](../../people/JHD.md), proposed `Object.propertyCount(target, options)` to avoid allocating an array just to count (`Object.keys(obj).length`, common across Angular, React, Node, Next.js), with options filtering by key type and enumerability. [MF](../../people/MF.md) saw a performance-only proposal that engines should optimize instead; [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) was convinced by the aggregate cost. [KG](../../people/KG.md) was extremely skeptical of the index/non-index distinction, and [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) noted spec and V8 notions of indices diverge; [MM](../../people/MM.md) and [MAH](../../people/MAH.md) saw the key use case as an O(1) check for non-index properties on array-likes.

The conclusion was Stage 1 (support from [DE](../../people/DE.md), [KG](../../people/KG.md), [DJM](../../people/DJM.md), [CDA](../../people/CDA.md), and [JSH](../../people/JSH.md)), with the skeptics' concerns as non-blockers.

## Explicit Resource Management

- wiki: [Explicit Resource Management](../../proposals/explicit-resource-management.md)
- proposal: [proposal-explicit-resource-management](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-explicit-resource-management)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1F4kLwEUvBmyyTWq06HQgiJypcCWm3uwOzVDzFQ0xauE/edit#slide=id.p)

[DLM](../../people/DLM.md) (Daniel Minor, Mozilla) reported that SpiderMonkey's implementation of [Explicit Resource Management](../../proposals/explicit-resource-management.md) is complete and in Nightly behind a flag, maintaining a runtime dispose list. The needs-consensus item: disallow `using` declarations in bare `case` statements, where switch fallthrough could skip disposal — without fallthrough, implementations can desugar `using` to try/finally. [RBN](../../people/RBN.md) filed the spec PR (rbuckton/ecma262#14), V8 is willing to rewrite its implementation, and support came from [MM](../../people/MM.md) (strongly), [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) ("switch is bad and it's ok if people can't use a new feature in it"), [PFC](../../people/PFC.md), and [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) (for Babel).

The conclusion was consensus to merge rbuckton/ecma262#14.

## Non-extensible applies to Private for stage 1, 2, 2.7

- wiki: [Non-extensible applies to Private](../../proposals/nonextensible-applies-to-private.md)
- proposal: [proposal-nonextensible-applies-to-private](https://github.com/syg/proposal-nonextensible-applies-to-private)
- slide: [keynote slides](https://github.com/syg/proposal-nonextensible-applies-to-private/blob/main/no-stamping-talks/non-extensible-applies-to-private.pdf)

[MM](../../people/MM.md) presented ([SYG](../../people/SYG.md) co-champion) a proposal extracted from [Stabilize](../../proposals/stabilize.md): adding a private field to a non-extensible object should throw a `TypeError` instead of silently succeeding. Today the behavior is counterintuitive; structs and composites need a fixed shape (extensibility plus return-override could force a shape change); and private-fields-as-syntax-WeakMap creates a hidden communications channel. The change is not backwards compatible: Google's usage counters show 0.000015% of page loads, six German websites (a Cadenza GIS deployment, Axial framework sites, a music festival site), and [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) is doing outreach. The proposal advanced rapidly through the stages in one session: Stage 1, then Stage 2 with [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) and [DE](../../people/DE.md) as reviewers (both signed off), then a conditional Stage 2.7 with [MF](../../people/MF.md)'s editor review pending ([KG](../../people/KG.md) and [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) approved).

The conclusion was Stage 1, Stage 2, and conditional Stage 2.7 on this day; [MF](../../people/MF.md)'s approval came later in the meeting, and the chairs confirmed on Day 4 (2025-04-17) that it officially reached Stage 2.7.

## Continuation of Object.propertyCount for stage 1 or 2

- wiki: [Object.propertyCount](../../proposals/object-propertycount.md)
- proposal: [object-property-count](https://github.com/ljharb/object-property-count)
- slide: [link](https://github.com/tc39/agendas/blob/main/2025/2025.04%20-%20Object.propertyCount%20slides.pdf)

Resuming after the stage grant, [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) argued counting properties and the arrays problem (sparse arrays, the index/non-index split) are different problems and asked to keep them separate; [JHD](../../people/JHD.md)'s "avoid performance cliffs" framing was judged too broad (dictionary mode would creep back in). The group settled on a burn-down list of use cases as the problem statement: [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) is "very enthusiastic about property counting… very skeptical about" the arrays part.

The conclusion was consensus to progress to Stage 1 on that narrowed problem statement, with the scope possibly narrowed further or split later.

## Continuation: Don't Remember Panicking

- wiki: [Don't Remember Panicking](../../proposals/dont-remember-panicking.md)
- proposal: [proposal-oom-fails-fast](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-oom-fails-fast/tree/master)
- slide: [link](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-oom-fails-fast/blob/master/panic-talks/dont-remember-panicking.pdf)

[SYG](../../people/SYG.md) asked what the host hook buys Agoric; [MM](../../people/MM.md) said it makes OOM discussable at all for the language and its formal semantics. [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) retracted his earlier "not a violation of the spec" — it is one, though one impossible to conform to. [ABO](../../people/ABO.md) pointed to the HTML spec's "killing scripts" section. On what browsers do today, [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) said Chrome kills the whole process (a dynamic agent cluster would be "pretty much unimplementable and nondeterministic") and [MLS](../../people/MLS.md) said Safari kills the web content process. [SYG](../../people/SYG.md) supports better specifying real-world resources, but via editorial means rather than a host hook that could become a configurable toggle.

The conclusion, per the speaker's summary: extensive discussion, the proposal stays at Stage 1, and there was general agreement that Ecma262 should more robustly specify real-world resource handling.
