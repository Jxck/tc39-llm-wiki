## Intl Era Monthcode for Stage 3

- wiki: [Intl Era/Month Code](../../proposals/intl-era-month-code.md)
- proposal: [proposal-intl-era-monthcode](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/)
- slide: [link](https://notes.igalia.com/p/Lne8U2E5K#/)
- [BAN](../../people/BAN.md) withdrew the Stage 3 request until January because of two late normative changes. He recapped the scope (supported calendars, era codes and aliases from CLDR, the reference-year range extended to 2035 for a rare Chinese leap month). test262 coverage, the last missing piece from September, has now been written (thanks to [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) and [JMN](../../people/JMN.md)).
- The two changes: [PR #72](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/pull/72) removes Ethiopian and ROC era aliases that CLDR is dropping, and [PR #89](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/pull/89) removes a spurious out-of-range era-year check that broke the Japanese imperial calendar. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) listed open Stage 3-milestone issues (#96, #57, #29, #60, #87, #90-#94), which were deferred to TG2.
- Conclusion: consensus on both normative changes. Review the open issues and return at the next plenary.

## Amount Stage 1 update

- wiki: [Amount](../../proposals/amount.md)
- proposal: [proposal-amount](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-amount)
- slide: [link](https://notes.igalia.com/p/tc39-november-2025-amount)
- [BAN](../../people/BAN.md): [Amount](../../proposals/amount.md) had been cut down to a value tagged with a unit and precision, with no arithmetic. Prompted by the HTML `<amount>` proposal discussed at TPAC, he proposed going back to unit conversion (a `convertTo`, starting with SI units, beyond CLDR). [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) said JS should offer unit conversion so the HTML element isn't abused for it.
- [WH](../../people/WH.md) wanted clarity on conversion versus display, which units, and precision, and suggested separating conversion from Amount. [KG](../../people/KG.md) said unit conversion would justify Amount as a first-class type (a mere unit carrier would not). [OFR](../../people/OFR.md) asked for a roadmap of related proposals, and CHU and [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) supported the direction.
- Speaker's summary: significant plenary support for returning Amount to a model that supports arithmetic and unit conversion, a widening of the earlier scope.
- Conclusion: research which units to include, and develop a roadmap for Amount relative to associated proposals (remains Stage 1).

## Intl Energy Units for Stage 1

- wiki: [Intl Energy Units](../../proposals/intl-energy-units.md)
- proposal: [proposal-intl-energy-units](https://github.com/johanrd/proposal-intl-energy-units)
- [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) presented on behalf of [BAN](../../people/BAN.md) (no slides): add `watt`, `kilowatt`, and `kilowatt-hour` from CLDR to `Intl.NumberFormat` for EV, battery, and electricity UIs. It is one of the most-requested ecma402 issues, and TG2 prefers small batches of units.
- [WH](../../people/WH.md) objected to the narrow scope that leaves out SI units such as joules. [MF](../../people/MF.md) and [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) supported narrow proposals. After hearing about the CLDR data dependency and binary-size cost ([SFC](../../people/SFC.md), [EAO](../../people/EAO.md)), [WH](../../people/WH.md) accepted limiting it to CLDR units.
- Conclusion: **reached Stage 1**. The list of units will be settled for Stage 2.

## `Object.getNonIndexStringProperties` for Stage 1 or 2

- wiki: [Object.getNonIndexStringProperties](../../proposals/object-get-non-index-string-properties.md)
- proposal: [array-get-non-index-string-properties](https://github.com/BridgeAR/array-get-non-index-string-properties)
- [RBR](../../people/RBR.md) (no slides) gave an update on the proposal that got Stage 1 in 2025-07 as `Array.getNonIndexStringProperties`. It has moved from Array to Object (for array-like and TypedArray inputs), and the options bag is gone (enumerable only). He did not plan to ask for advancement and proposed a usage survey.
- [MF](../../people/MF.md) and [ACE](../../people/ACE.md) said surveys are weak evidence and asked for concrete existing code (paving cowpaths). [KG](../../people/KG.md) was unconvinced by the logging and assert use cases and wants a strong motivation before Stage 2. [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) argued it is needed for logging and validation, and [MAH](../../people/MAH.md) and [WH](../../people/WH.md) cited validation of TypedArrays with no extra properties.
- Conclusion: encouraged by the committee, it was recorded as advancing to **Stage 1** under the new name (it was already Stage 1 as `Array.getNonIndexStringProperties`). [KG](../../people/KG.md)'s motivation concerns would block Stage 2.

## Class composition: Past, present, and future

- proposal: [explainer](https://github.com/webplatformco/project-class-composition)
- slide: [link](https://webplatform.design/projects/class-composition/talk/)
- [LVU](../../people/LVU.md) surveyed the use cases (class modularity, polymorphic procedural / OO APIs, traits vs identity, for example `EventTarget`) and other languages (interfaces, mixins, traits, Eiffel renaming). She also covered userland patterns (subclass factories, delegation, prototype mutation) and existing proposals (first-class protocols, maximally minimal mixins, [decorators](../../proposals/decorators.md)). She proposed separating "tightly coupled" from "loosely coupled" use cases.
- [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) and [MF](../../people/MF.md) questioned splitting large classes. [RBN](../../people/RBN.md) mentioned C# partial classes and [WH](../../people/WH.md) Swift extensions. [MF](../../people/MF.md) insisted that automatic conflict resolution is dangerous for loosely coupled composition.
- Conclusion: interest in the problems, without consensus on specifics. Keep working on it.

## Class spread syntax for Stage 1

- proposal: [proposal-class-spread](https://github.com/LeaVerou/proposal-class-spread)
- slide: [link](https://webplatform.design/projects/class-composition/talk/#class-spread-syntax)
- [LVU](../../people/LVU.md) proposed `...Other` inside a class body to copy members by descriptor, order-sensitive and without touching the inheritance chain, for both classes and plain objects.
- [WH](../../people/WH.md) said it breaks the abstraction of every existing class (adding a private field or method could break spreaders). [ACE](../../people/ACE.md) wanted classes to opt in, [MAH](../../people/MAH.md) doubted classes can be a spread source at all, and [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) rejected anything that encourages mixins. [MM](../../people/MM.md) would support only a broader problem statement whose answer might be "existing patterns suffice".
- A reworded statement ("exploring ways to improve sharing code across classes") got support from [MM](../../people/MM.md), [MAH](../../people/MAH.md), and [JHD](../../people/JHD.md), but [CM](../../people/CM.md) objected as too vague, and [WH](../../people/WH.md) seconded.
- Conclusion: class spread syntax remains at Stage 0.

## Class field introspection for Stage 1

- proposal: [proposal-class-field-introspection](https://github.com/LeaVerou/proposal-class-field-introspection)
- slide: [link](https://webplatform.design/projects/class-composition/talk/#class-field-introspection)
- [LVU](../../people/LVU.md) argued that public fields are class API and should be introspectable from outside the class without instantiating it (a strawman Symbol returning field metadata, possibly with initializers). [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) agreed that the class shape should be statically defined. [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) disagreed that fields are the instance shape.
- [MAH](../../people/MAH.md) and [MAG](../../people/MAG.md) objected to exposing initializers, and [RBN](../../people/RBN.md) noted that [decorators](../../proposals/decorators.md) already see fields. [MM](../../people/MM.md) objected on refactoring hazard grounds (moving constructor assignments to field declarations must not change the public API), and [GCL](../../people/GCL.md) and [JSL](../../people/JSL.md) also objected to the problem statement. There was some support for an explicit, opt-in declaration mechanism instead.
- Conclusion: public field introspection remains at Stage 0.

## Continuation: Declarations in Conditionals for Stage 2

- [DRO](../../people/DRO.md) (no advancement requested) proposed sidestepping the else-visibility question by making references to the binding from the `else` branch a SyntaxError, leaving the decision for later. [ACE](../../people/ACE.md) liked it, [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) asked why not TDZ, and [JRL](../../people/JRL.md) saw it as a lint concern.
- [KG](../../people/KG.md) argued that `using` forces the answer: a resource bound in the `if` header must be disposed before `else` runs, since Rust's old behavior caused bugs. [WH](../../people/WH.md) concluded that the binding can therefore never be in scope in `else`. [RBN](../../people/RBN.md) countered that excluding `else` is a refactoring hazard.
- Conclusion: with `using` now supported, the else-visibility question has to be answered now, because it determines when disposal happens. No advancement.

## Continuation: Composites comparator choice

- wiki: [Composite Keys](../../proposals/composite-keys.md)
- [ACE](../../people/ACE.md) ran two non-binding temperature checks. On "if we intern, should we throw on leaky WeakMap/WeakSet insertion?": 17 strong positive, 2 positive, 3 indifferent, 1 unconvinced. On "do we lean towards interning?": 10 strong positive, 4 positive, 4 confused, 2 indifferent, 2 unconvinced ([MM](../../people/MM.md) later withdrew his vote).
- [WH](../../people/WH.md) asked how non-interned composites would behave in WeakMaps. [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) wanted a "can be weakly held" predicate instead of try/catch.
- Conclusion: throwing for invalid Composite WeakMap keys and interning composites each got the most support. [ACE](../../people/ACE.md) will keep researching (more benchmarks) and return with more information.
