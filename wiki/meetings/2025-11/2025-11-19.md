## Async iterator helpers recap/update/polyfill

- proposal: [proposal-async-iterator-helpers](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-async-iterator-helpers/)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1nQvAwvOQ0gDJ-eLIVIy3rYrlZj3qGvPHCeH_Veba2cc/edit?usp=sharing)
- [KG](../../people/KG.md) restated the design principles: values are not lost, results are race-free up to the first rejection or `{done: true}`, and a consumer that has seen `{done: true}` stops pulling from the underlying iterator. He leans toward dropping that last principle because of the complexity it adds. The helpers stay ordered, so they cannot reach maximal throughput.
- He raised the need for a cleanup hook (both after and before the underlying iterator's cleanup, for example with `AbortController`). [CM](../../people/CM.md) wanted it in the first version, while [MF](../../people/MF.md) and [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) thought it could come later.
- Conclusion: no conclusion sought. Interested parties are invited to continue on GitHub.

## Iterator Join for stage 1, 2, or 2.7

- wiki: [Iterator Join](../../proposals/iterator-join.md)
- proposal: [proposal-iterator-join](https://github.com/bakkot/proposal-iterator-join)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1jclIOSbSdyepVCiYXQ4eXc2Uh8FBnBKnFOtsx12N3r0/edit?usp=sharing)
- [KG](../../people/KG.md) presented a new proposal, `Iterator.prototype.join`. Joining an iterator into a string is common (thousands of `Array.from(x.keys()).join()` hits on GitHub), and `.reduce` or converting to an array both have downsides. It deliberately copies `Array.prototype.join`, including ToString coercion and the default `","` separator.
- [MF](../../people/MF.md) disliked the name, the coercion, and the optional separator, but supported it as "the best we can do". [RBN](../../people/RBN.md) raised the overloaded term "join" and unbounded iterators. [KG](../../people/KG.md) and [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) answered that people already materialize arrays to do this. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) supported it.
- Conclusion: Stage 1, then Stage 2 ([WH](../../people/WH.md), [MF](../../people/MF.md), and [JHD](../../people/JHD.md); [MF](../../people/MF.md) and [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) as reviewers), then **Stage 2.7** with editor approval (new → 2.7).

## Composites comparator choice

- wiki: [Composite Keys](../../proposals/composite-keys.md)
- proposal: [proposal-composites](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-composites/)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1ByfhAVVeEfeBj2g8TkP6f55hSmq8_8tevUxuRQLfISg/)
- [ACE](../../people/ACE.md) asked no stage question and presented the core design choice. Unique objects whose equality only some APIs (Map / Set / includes) recognize, or interned objects that work with `===`? He benchmarked both in V8 against `JSON.stringify`. Interning costs more at creation but pays back as soon as a key is used twice.
- The downside of interning is GC semantics. A composite with no object inside can never be collected, so putting it in a WeakMap or WeakSet should either throw or leak. [GCL](../../people/GCL.md), [JAD](../../people/JAD.md), [WH](../../people/WH.md), [KM](../../people/KM.md), and [RBN](../../people/RBN.md) favored throwing (in line with the "forgeable" rule from Symbols as WeakMap keys), while [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) did not mind the leak. [WH](../../people/WH.md) raised `-0` and NaN communication channels ([ACE](../../people/ACE.md): normalize). [MAG](../../people/MAG.md) warned interning may be very hard in SpiderMonkey. [KM](../../people/KM.md) and [RBN](../../people/RBN.md) also brought up user-defined equality.
- Conclusion: none on this day (continued on Day 3).

## Concurrency Control stage 1 update

- wiki: [Concurrency Control](../../proposals/concurrency-control.md)
- proposal: [proposal-concurrency-control](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-concurrency-control)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1WoEqtVKEJgIhyTbOO2aWbNKLCFNPXHMb0ZW3gMFpyvU)
- [MF](../../people/MF.md) presented the current design. It adds an optional concurrency argument (an integer or a Governor) to the six `AsyncIterator.prototype` consumers, `AsyncIterator.prototype.limit(n)`, the Governor / GovernorToken protocol (tokens are disposable), and a built-in `CountingGovernor` (a semaphore). Concurrent task queues are now out of scope. Popular npm libraries and their download counts were shown as motivation.
- Before Stage 2, the proposal needs AbortSignal integration and answers to its open questions (idle listeners, throttling, a `Governor.all` to avoid deadlocks, acquire parameters). [KG](../../people/KG.md) wanted the name `Semaphore` back and proposed improving AbortController / AbortSignal in WHATWG and pulling a subset into JS, which [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) welcomed.
- Conclusion: the committee was updated and gave feedback (remains Stage 1).

## `Object.propertyCount` for Stage 2

- wiki: [Object.propertyCount](../../proposals/object-propertycount.md)
- proposal: [proposal-object-property-count](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-object-property-count)
- [RBR](../../people/RBR.md) (slides not shared) dropped the options-bag design in favor of separate methods, asking first for `Object.keysLength()`, with `getOwnPropertyNames` / Symbol counts as later steps. [KM](../../people/KM.md), [MAG](../../people/MAG.md), [LVU](../../people/LVU.md), and [MAH](../../people/MAH.md) preferred separate methods, since allocating an options bag defeats the performance goal, and nobody wanted the options bag.
- [KG](../../people/KG.md) supported only the keys count and criticized the missing materials as an exception to the agenda-deadline rule. [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) asked about naming (`keysLength` vs `keyCount`). [MAH](../../people/MAH.md) argued for non-enumerable counts on TypedArrays.
- Conclusion: `Object.propertyCount` stays at Stage 1. `Object.key[sLength|Count]` is split out as a **new Stage 2 proposal**, and its name must be decided before Stage 2.7.

## WHATWG Stages

- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1I-3g1QFGaiazy98hf0RisfH5f0BVn6SRH5lfxxXJ2g4/edit?usp=sharing)
- [CZW](../../people/CZW.md) explained the opt-in WHATWG stage process (since April 2025; Stages 1-4 with no 2.7, explainer-based, WPT required at Stage 3) and asked how TC39 proposals with major web integration should line up with it. [AsyncContext](../../proposals/async-context.md) is already at WHATWG Stage 1, and [ShadowRealm](../../proposals/shadowrealm.md) and [ESM phase imports](../../proposals/esm-phase-imports.md) are candidates.

[JSL](../../people/JSL.md) warned against tying TC39 to WHATWG, which disregards server-side runtimes. [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) said he wants a merged HTML PR before implementing [Import Bytes](../../proposals/import-bytes.md) / [Import Text](../../proposals/import-text.md).
- Conclusion: the WHATWG stage process will not be an official criterion for advancement in TC39. TC39 Stage 2.7 should be a strong signal for the HTML side to move ahead.

## Spec-defined or Implementation-defined limits

- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1NsezmIvFSr4qH4EXnh-cqOjikiUx196feKNM797m6fw/edit?slide=id.p#slide=id.p)
- [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) compared spec-defined limits (array length, Intl mathematical value), hybrids (ArrayBuffer's 2^53-1 plus an implementation-defined RangeError), and implementation-defined limits (BigInt, strings, where engines throw, crash, or hang differently). He asked when to use each and whether spec-defined minimums should be favored.
- [MM](../../people/MM.md) and [MAH](../../people/MAH.md) restated the "OOM must fail fast" position. [OFR](../../people/OFR.md), [KM](../../people/KM.md), and [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) said throwing on limits is relied on by the web. [WH](../../people/WH.md) and [MAG](../../people/MAG.md) supported adding optional RangeError throws where limits can be hit.
- Speaker's summary: fair support for explicitly allowing engines to throw on memory limits, for wording that lets them throw not only when the allocator fails, and for spec-defined minimums in specific proposals.
- Conclusion: future proposals that involve unbounded operations should refer to these principles.

## Module-declaration like proposals in other areas of the web

- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1DQkU3Bgqp0M9afRALa21GWNyAmyCy4A5lUhzdQe_WAs/edit?slide=id.p#slide=id.p)
- [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) reported from TPAC on web-platform proposals that bring "nested files" or modules to other languages: HTML modules (HTML imports), inline CSS modules defined in HTML with global specifiers that go through the import map, and CSS `@sheet` rules. He compared them with TC39's module declarations.
- [LVU](../../people/LVU.md) and [JAD](../../people/JAD.md) worried about mutable, declarative specifiers (changing the attribute or content after import) and about whether the use cases are really resource-loading workarounds. [JAD](../../people/JAD.md) said most of the motivation is declarative shadow DOM styling. [GB](../../people/GB.md) urged continued TC39 involvement.
- Conclusion: none (informational). [NRO](../../people/NRO.md) will keep working with the relevant groups.

## Comparisons

- wiki: [Comparisons](../../proposals/comparisons.md)
- proposal: [proposal-comparisons](https://github.com/JakobJingleheimer/proposal-comparisons)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1tu8yK57yMDdBsD4FgDX7GuRndLZfqvrukEG_AukYeJU/view)
- [JSH](../../people/JSH.md) (with new co-champion [RGN](../../people/RGN.md)) returned with a single comparison function that returns `true` or an iterator of "deviations" (path, expected / actual, reason), with `first` / `all` modes, leaves compared by SameValueZero. He asked for Stage 1.
- [WH](../../people/WH.md), [MF](../../people/MF.md), and [JAD](../../people/JAD.md) saw it as userland or dev-dependency material. CHU questioned the shift from equality to deviations, [MAH](../../people/MAH.md) worried about encouraging equality on mutable data, and [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) and [RKG](../../people/RKG.md) asked for a clear problem statement and why it belongs in the language. [JHD](../../people/JHD.md) and [JSL](../../people/JSL.md) supported Stage 1, and [MF](../../people/MF.md) said it was not ready.
- Conclusion: [Comparisons](../../proposals/comparisons.md) remains at Stage 0.
