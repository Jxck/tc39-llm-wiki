The motivation for a native version is that userland implementations are deliberately "incorrect" for performance reasons.

- Conclusion: the motivation was broadly accepted (many voices asking for a native solution), and a continuation was set the same day so a written statement for the vote could be prepared.

## An introduction to the EU CRA & how it matters to all of you

- slide: [link](https://hosted.akiro.se/2026/Ecma/TC39/Cyber%20Resilience%20Act%20primer.pdf)
- AKI gave an overview of the EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA). Selling a product on the EU single market requires compliance. Software, and products that contain it, are in scope (pure SaaS and websites are out of scope and fall under other regimes such as NIS2). The core is a risk-based approach: 14 essential cybersecurity requirements, and documenting a vulnerability-handling policy.
- Technical documentation needs an SBOM (CycloneDX, which Ecma is standardizing, can be used), a diagram of component relationships, a risk assessment, and the basis for the support period. The main text takes effect at the end of December 2027, but vulnerability handling and reporting have to be in place by September 2026.
- Vulnerability response: an early warning to ENISA within 24 hours for a severe incident or an actively exploited vulnerability, a notification within 72 hours, and a root-cause analysis within 14 or 30 days. Sharing a fix upstream in a machine-readable form (for example a PR) is a legal requirement.
- Open source has zero legal obligation unless it is accompanied by economic activity, and the CRA is not aimed at individual developers (scale is the axis of judgment). The Linux Foundation, Eclipse, OpenJS, and similar bodies can qualify as open-source stewards, but ECMA-262 is currently neither a product nor an open-source project, so Ecma has no obligation. Vulnerabilities in the spec itself are the product side's duty to report ([JHD](../../people/JHD.md), as TG3 convener, added that there is still an ethical responsibility).
- Conclusion (Speaker's Summary): the CRA affects almost every product company. Open source is protected from penalties, but in practice aligning with the cybersecurity requirements is desirable. On SHN's proposal, later plenaries will keep a 30-minute follow-up slot.

## agreeing to consider impact of RegExp proposals to linear implementations

- slide: [link](https://aurele-barriere.github.io/ecma_amsterdam_handout.pdf)
- [AUR](../../people/AUR.md), with [CPC](../../people/CPC.md), [MF](../../people/MF.md), and MBH, asked the committee to agree to consider the effect of RegExp proposals on linear-time matching implementations. From a formal-methods and verification standpoint, they showed that a JS regex without backreferences can be matched in linear time.
- They emphasized that the ECMAScript spec is particularly well suited to linear-time matching, and that ECMAScript is the only language for which a way to handle every lookaround in linear time is known (a property the other major languages do not have), although quantifiers need some ad-hoc adjustment.
- Against the background of how common ReDoS vulnerabilities are, the motivation was a facility that lets the user control or judge linearity. Several delegates reported that they already restrain their own use of regex because of the risk of catastrophic backtracking.
- Conclusion: broad support for considering the effect of new proposals on linearity. Consensus that future regex proposals include an impact assessment of regex-matching complexity. A non-blocking concern was raised that "complexity is hard to analyze," and it was settled that non-linearity is not an automatic reason to reject. [AUR](../../people/AUR.md) / [CPC](../../people/CPC.md) / [MF](../../people/MF.md) will bring back linearity-related proposals: stating a preference for [linear matching](../../proposals/linear-matching.md), running linearly, judging whether linear execution is possible, a time limit, and so on.

## Tooling for navigating the ECMA-262 specification and reviewing API proposals

- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/19iIbMpnV1mxhBcKZAdxd8KlNNpimgpcDefwssylQfuU/edit?usp=sharing)
- MBH and ATI presented, as a TG5 work item, an IDE-like tool for navigating the ECMA-262 specification. It treats the spec's pseudocode as code (ESMeta's premise) and covers about 1,100 abstract operations and built-in algorithms.
- An experimental tool based on JetBrains MPS, focused on reading the existing spec rather than authoring it. ESMeta parses the pseudocode into an AST and provides IDE features such as go-to-definition and diff, while imitating the look of the normative document.
- The demo loaded the 2023, 2024, and 2025 editions and opened an algorithm from a folder structure that corresponds to the table of contents.
- Conclusion: none (an informational presentation).
